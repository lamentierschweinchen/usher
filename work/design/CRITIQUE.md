# Critique: the mini-games

**P**: phone player, never read Poe. **D**: designer.

## 0 · Tarn: spot the difference
**P:** "One difference? That's a continue button."
**D:** Right mood, no depth, no fun on its own. Cutting *Seven Differences* to one find lost its best idea: the puzzle cheats. Fine ink is too small to tap on a phone.
**Problem:** It tells you to look down instead of tricking you into it.
**Challenge:** 60–90 seconds, five large finds, and one lie the player catches unaided (a circle un-circles itself). They leave distrusting the top half, unprompted.

## 1 · Door: sliding stones
**P:** "I skip sliding puzzles. I'd poke till it solved and never notice her."
**D:** Tedious and shallow, with the set's best image. A random 3×3 needs 20+ moves at best; players stumble into solving. She's scenery, and it's a second water reveal in a row.
**Problem:** Her being the gap doesn't change how you play.
**Challenge:** Drag *her*, not the stones, along the fissure: a hand-made scramble, 12 moves or fewer, one rule only the water shows. The player realises they've walked her to the door.

## 2 · Studio: whack-a-mole
**P:** "Speed! Funny for forty seconds, then it's tapping."
**D:** Most fun on its own. But eight pests on one tap is one verb in eight costumes; WarioWare asks for a new gesture each time. Her "one more click" taps grind, and there's no climax.
**Problem:** The first minute is the whole game; her tap is just another target.
**Challenge:** Give each pest its own gesture (draw the drape, pinch the rose, hold the band), and make hushing her tap the game's most rewarding act. The player must *want* to do it.

## 3 · Burial: tidying
**P:** "Shut the lid, it opens, shut it again. Got it. Can I go?"
**D:** Excellent mood (the blush, the smile), no depth. *A Little to the Left* hides a logic puzzle in every tidy; this hides a loop, and repeats the studio's twist.
**Problem:** No decision and no puzzle; the player is a hinge.
**Challenge:** Replace it (see Kill or keep). If it stays, every undoing is a new puzzle, and the player may leave one screw out, which pays off in the flight.

## 4 · Nights: explain it away
**P:** "Match word to label? It's a vocab test. What's catalepsy?"
**D:** Funniest voice, but four nights of one task drag. The author's warning, keyword matching, is the design. Scripted stamp-outs mean the ending happens to you.
**Problem:** Fit is binary and labelled: no judgment, no cost.
**Challenge:** Make it *Papers, Please*. Each noise has details (where, when, how loud); each excuse has limits ("the draperies" can't reach below the floor). Stamps are scarce, each night adds a rule, and the player picks what goes unexplained. Gloss every excuse.

## 5 · Trist: 8-bit quest
**P:** "Ha, a NES game! How do I jump on a phone?"
**D:** Three scripted beats are a cutscene with buttons. It fits the mood only as one-colour woodcut, not neon. Eyes stay on the action, missing the reflection.
**Problem:** The best reveal sits where nobody looks.
**Challenge:** Put the truth in the controls. At the second blow the screen flips: you're Madeline clawing out of the vault, the dragon is Roderick, and the player strikes the last blow.

## 6 · Flight
**P:** "So I hold right till it ends?"
**D:** One line in the spine; a second runner after the trist, with no decision in it.
**Problem:** It's a cutscene.
**Challenge:** The only input is *look back* (Poe's narrator "turned to see"): the tarn's "look down" returned. Earlier choices change what you see and how long the causeway holds.

## Across the run
**Verbs:** tap, slide, tap, drag, match, jump, run. Too much tapping; two body-movers end it, two top-versus-bottom comparisons open it. Neighbours must not share an input.
**Pacing:** Two slow puzzles open it; phone players leave by minute four. Hook within 60 seconds, rest at the burial, peak once at the trist.
**Length:** As specced, 15–22 minutes (the door and four nights swing it). Budget: tarn 1, door 2, studio 2.5, burial 1.5, nights 3, trist 2.5, flight 1, walking 3: about 16.5.
**Tricks:** Four chapters tell one joke: you've been silencing her. By the third, players see it coming. "Constantly tricks you" means a new trick per chapter: it cheats, it steers you, it rewards the wrong thing, it swaps who you are, it follows you.
**Confession:** A gimmick as written: renaming a forced score is a punchline, not guilt. It lands only if players chased the numbers and could have scored lower. At least three chapters need a playable alternative (spare the tap, skip a screw, leave a noise unexplained), and the readback should quote those choices. Cut "Usher: 0"; it's unreadable.

## Kill or keep
- **Replace the burial's tidying** with the stealth runner-up, recast. "We two alone bore it to its rest," and Roderick buries her at home to dodge her "medical men". Carry the coffin past the physician of "low cunning" while a second set of footsteps copies your route: a new verb (route and timing), a new trick. The lid becomes a 20-second beat: four screws, the blush. Unbuilt, so the swap is free.
- **Keep** the other six, rebuilt to their challenges. Short on time? Merge trist and flight first.
