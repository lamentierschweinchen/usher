# Story: A Sojourn of Some Weeks

## The arcs

**The guest (you)**
1. Arrives with one cure, cheerfulness, and an explanation for everything.
2. Gets very good at it: draperies, storm, catalepsy, coincidence.
3. His last explanation is his truest. She is there anyway.

**Roderick**
1. Your oldest friend, overjoyed to see you, unable to bear your coat.
2. Afraid of everything, then of one thing, under the floor.
3. He has heard her all along, and sent for you because you never hear anything.

**Madeline**
1. Never speaks; crosses rooms as if you were the ghost.
2. Felt in gaps: the empty square, a *tap*, a lid that won't sit flush, the tarn running ahead.
3. Her noises precede your blows. At the door, she alone never explains herself.

## The letter

*(Roderick's hand: blotted, crossed out, underlined twice.)*

> My dear old friend — come. I am ill: the senses. Light cuts. Roses shout. Bring *nothing that rings*. Bring your cheerfulness; you always had extra. My sister is ~~dying~~ ~~quiet~~ resting. The house is well. The house is *very* well. Come within the fortnight.
> Your best, your only, R.U.
> P.S. Don't look in the tarn. Everyone does.

### I. A Somewhat Childish Experiment *(tarn)*
The house regards you with vacant, eye-like windows. You regard it back. Someone has to.
- **Servant:** "Shall I stable him for some weeks, sir?"
  **You:** "A night or two."
- **A peasant:** "The House of Usher, sir: the stones or the family?"
  **You:** "Is there a difference?"
- **Your horse:** *(will not look in the water.)*
  **You:** "Sensible animal."

**Out:** All differences accounted for. The crack is only in the water, and you aren't staying in the water.
**Tarn:** a lit window, dark above. Someone in it looks down, as you did.

### II. A Mere Different Arrangement *(door)*
Every stone is sound, and none is where it belongs. A sensible man could fix that.
- **The valet, of stealthy step:** *(has been behind you some time. Says nothing, at length.)*
  **You:** "Good Lord."
- **The physician:** "If the sister dies, send for me. Before anyone does anything."
  **You:** "What would anyone do?"
- **Servant:** "The family don't use the door, sir."
  **You:** "Then how do they get out?"

**Out:** The last stone slides home. The door opens, very nearly by itself.
**Tarn:** the gap you kept pushing about, standing in the doorway. It has a face.

### III. The Cheerfulness of My Society *(studio)*
Roderick rises from the sofa, overjoyed, in a whisper.
- **Roderick:** "You came! Is that wool? It's terribly loud."
  **You:** "It's a coat, Roderick."
- **Roderick:** "It's an incurable family evil. It'll pass by Thursday."
  **You:** "Then I'll stay till Friday."
- **Madeline:** *(passes slowly through, without having noticed you.)*
  **Roderick:** "We don't mention her. It tires her."

**Out:** Everything is hushed. Roderick thanks you, and goes on listening to it.
**Tarn:** Madeline lying down on a long table in the cellar. Very neatly. A little early.

### IV. A Harmless Precaution *(burial)*
His sister is no more. You help with the arrangements; you're good at arrangements.
- **Roderick:** "A fortnight in the vault. The doctors are so *eager*."
  **You:** "I don't feel at liberty to dispute it."
- **The physician:** "Might I just see her?"
  **The valet:** *(closes the door, in silence.)*
- **You:** "She's blushing."
  **Roderick:** "It's usual. In our family."

**Out:** Four screws home. The lid is flush, more or less.
**Tarn:** the lid, leaning against the vault wall like a borrowed umbrella.

### V. The Seventh or Eighth Night *(nights)*
Your bed is directly over the vault. A coincidence, and you explain it as one.
- **Roderick, roaming:** "Do you hear —"
  **You:** "The draperies, old man."
- **The valet's stealthy step:** *(passes your door.)*
  **Another, stealthier:** *(passes underneath.)*
- **Roderick, with a lamp:** "And you have not *seen* it? Stay. You shall."
  **You:** "I'll get my book."

**Out:** You run out of stamps before it runs out of noises.
**Tarn:** the iron door ajar, and someone on the stair, resting on every step.

### VI. One of Your Favorite Romances *(trist)*
You read aloud the silliest book in the house. You're the hero. It's wonderful.
- **The hermit:** "Nobody's home!"
  **Ethelred:** "Then nobody will mind." *(crack, rip)*
- **The dragon:** "Please. I'm very sensitive to noise."
  **Ethelred:** "Sorry." *(bonk)*
- **The shield:** *(falls before you reach it, with a mighty great and terrible ringing.)*
  **You:** "I didn't touch it."

**Out:** Shield won. You look up, delighted. Roderick has turned his chair to face the door.
**Tarn:** the dragon had her brother's face. The doors of this room are already open.

### VII. A Thousand Waters *(flight)*
Roderick finally speaks. He's been listening all along.
- **Roderick:** "I heard her days ago. I sent for you because you never hear anything."
  **You:** "Oh, Roderick."
- **Roderick:** "Madman! She now stands without the door!"
  **You:** "That's only the wind."
- **The doors:** *(open. It is the wind.)*
  **Madeline:** *(is there anyway.)*

**Out:** A long tumultuous shouting, like a thousand waters. Then the tarn shuts, sullenly and silently, like a book.
**Tarn:** the House of Usher, upright and whole, every window lit. One of them is yours.

## The ending

**The confession.** *An Account of My Sojourn*, in ink, corrected in red in Roderick's phrase.

- Differences found: 7. *Say, rather:* one window uncounted.
- Stones set in order: 8. *Say, rather:* the ninth was her.
- Taps hushed: 41. *Say, rather:* she knocked 41 times.
- Screws turned: 4. *Say, rather:* 4 in, 4 out.
- Noises explained: 12. *Say, rather:* 12 of 13.
- Dragons slain: 1. *Say, rather:* brothers, 1.
- Days stayed: 9. *Say, rather:* some weeks.

Last line: **Cheerfulness of my society: considerable.** The red starts *say, rather:* and stops.

**The last card.** The picture turns over: the reflected house now stands upright.

> The tarn closed sullenly and silently over the House of Usher.
> The House of Usher closed sullenly and silently over the tarn.

## Connoisseur layer

1. **The P.S.** Looking down is the narrator's "childish experiment" [u05]; the house lives by "reduplication in the still waters" [u26]. The tutorial is the spell.
2. **The physician.** His "low cunning" [u07] is why the narrator agrees to the vault [u28]. Knowers distrust the right man.
3. **"It's usual. In our family."** No "enduring branch", only "very temporary variation" [u04]: a twin per generation goes downstairs. Portraits hang in pairs, one face painted out.
4. **The echo runs backwards.** Poe's noises follow the romance [u40]; ours come a beat early. The book echoes her.
5. **The sensitive dragon.** Roderick's torments [u11] were the studio's enemies. In the Trist he's the loudest thing in the book.
6. **The gust.** Poe's narrator is right about the gust [u49]. So are you. It makes no difference.

## Liberties taken

- Madeline is felt in every chapter.
- The physician is right; twin burials are a custom (hinted).
- Roderick sent for you on purpose.
- The Trist is a game you win; its noises come first.
- The house comes up the right way.
