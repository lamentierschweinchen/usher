A Sojourn of Some Weeks

a game in two acts, after Poe

A Burial of Some Weeks

You come to a strange house to cheer up an old friend, and you're very good at explaining things away. For an evening the house, its reflection, your diary and everyone in it mislead you, each in one consistent way you can learn to see through. Then the house turns over. You become the woman you explained away, playing from under the floor against a recording of your own evening, and what she does decides how it ends.

This is the bible of 27 October laid out as one journey. My read and the seven things to settle are at the end: jump there.

The story

Three moves

Act one

A Sojourn of Some Weeks · 52 min

You are the guest. Roderick Usher's frantic letter brings you to a house over a black tarn, and the tarn shows the house upside down and not quite the same. You play Poe's tale as the guest would tell it: you spot what's wrong, then explain it away, and your diary, the Account, writes it down. His sister Madeline crosses rooms without noticing you. She collapses, is laid in the vault, and you help screw down the lid. Some nights later the house falls and you ride away. All evening, you were being recorded.

The hinge

1 min

The Account's last line writes itself: Cheerfulness of my society: considerable. Before you can tap, a back-slanted red hand strikes it. The curtain drops, the stage turns by itself, and Nevertheless. appears on the turned side. call 4

Act two

A Burial of Some Weeks · 28 min

You are Madeline, under the floor, in the reflected house. Every chapter turns over. You play against a ghost of your own evening, which answers you with your old excuses. Each turn opens on a reveal and strikes one line of the Account, rewritten in her red. What she does, and what you did above, picks one of five endings.

What really happened

The secret

The Ushers come in twins, and the house stands only while one of each pair lives below, in its reflection. At "the fortnight" the chosen twin is laid in the vault with the lid unscrewed: a fortnight to change their mind, then the way closes. A guest is always invited, to carry the coffin and to write it all up as a death. This time it was Roderick's turn. He sent his sister, call 1 and you screwed the lid down, taking her fortnight. She came up anyway, early. With nobody below, the house falls.

Left open on purpose: did she go down for him meaning to stay, or to make him follow? And is the valet the house?

The shape

Eighty-two minutes, to scale

Act one runs above the waterline and act two below it, in the same chapter order; the red bar is the hinge. Pale blocks are the scenes between chapters: the letter and arrival, interludes, the gallery, her collapse, the supper, the library, the Account, and at the very end the ending slots.

The journey

Seven chapters, each played twice

Each plate is one chapter: its act-one half above the waterline, its act-two half below. In play, all of act one comes first. Every chapter teaches one liar, ends on one choice, and in act two opens on a reveal that rewrites one line of the Account.

The first interlude

2 min · above
The interlude verbs, taught here. Pin a difference in both halves, then explain it with an excuse card (the draperies, the storm, catalepsy, family custom, merely electrical phenomena, coincidence) or leave it be, and it goes on the Account's back page, Unaccounted for. Dither ten seconds and the guest explains it for you, in paler ink marked m. In the water's window a figure looks up. On the stair, the physician, lit from below: "If the sister dies, send for me. Below, each interlude is about a minute: Madeline walks the reflected house, where your excuses are things in her way and what you left be is an open door. Knock where an excuse can't reach, and the wall gives.

My read

Three things to fix before it's settled

1. Act two has no clock, and the lore has a gap there

The bible never says when act two happens. Madeline plays the tarn, the door and the studio from below, yet in act one she was still walking about above at those times. The lore has the matching hole: if one twin must always live below, who was down there before her burial?

I'd fix itwith one rule that Poe already hands us: catalepsy is the custom. The twin below leaves the body above in a trance, "a trance that passes for death". Madeline went down when the fortnight began, before you arrived, which is why she crosses rooms without noticing you. Act two is that same evening from her side, so every odd thing you saw above was her doing it from below, a little early. The collapse and the burial are the house laying out her body for the guest to write up, and your fourth screw is what really shuts her out. The hinge can carry it in five words: The same evening, from below.

2. The premise needs to fit in three sentences

The secret has about eight rules: twins, one below, the fortnight, the unscrewed lid, the guest who writes, whose turn, the physician's twelve days, a guest every generation. The bible says every player gets the premise but doesn't say which part is the premise.

I'd fix itby naming the three sentences a first-timer must be able to say by the hinge, and guaranteeing each on act one's main path. The Ushers are twins, and one of them always lives below, in the reflection. Madeline went down, and was laid in the vault with the lid unscrewed so she could come back. I screwed it shut. Everything else is reward for looking, never a requirement.

3. The mirror arrives at minute 53

The game's best idea, playing her against your own evening, starts after almost an hour. The title, her crossings, her three edits and the tarn's early pictures promise it, and the hinge only lands if act one was long enough to be worth recording.

I'd keepthe shape and let your first playthrough judge it. If act one drags, cut act one before adding anything, rather than moving the turn earlier.
