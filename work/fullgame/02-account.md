# Blueprint 02 · The Account

## 1. Name and pitch
**An Account of My Sojourn.** You write down your stay; the house, the tarn and everyone in it correct you, each in their own way, and whatever you write, the house keeps.

## 2. The shape (85 min)
Letter and arrival 2. **Act I, *A Sojourn of Some Weeks*:** seven days of morning in the house (2–3), chapter game (5–7) and evening at the Account (1–2): tarn 7, door 8, studio 9, burial 10, nights 11, trist 10, flight 9 = **64**. First confession 3. **Act II, *A Burial of Some Weeks*:** 14. Last page 2.

## 3. Between chapters: gather, write, be read
- **Morning, gather.** Walk the cutaway; tap above to *see*, below to *glance*. Answer people with an excuse you own ("The draperies, old man") or "Go on." Each look or line pockets a **slip** (what, where, when, whose hand).
- **Evening, write.** Two or three sentences with blanks: "On the stair I met ___, who ___." Drag in a slip, stamp it with an excuse, or leave it out.
- **Supper, be read.** Roderick hears it and answers line by line.
- **Night, kept.** The house rearranges itself to match your page.

The verb is *write it down*; the house is your reader.

## 4. Truth and deceit
Each witness lies one way, with one tell.
- **You**, by explanation: an excuse fits by blurring one field. *Tell:* the blur shows under the stamp.
- **The house**, by keeping: overnight it edits itself to match your page, never inventing. *Tell:* edits are fresh-cut, lamp-rimmed for a day.
- **The tarn** leans true, but *inverts* (rising in the water is going down), *ripples* (in wind it shows the next moment) and *flatters* (it shows the looker as he'd like to be). *Tells:* rings mean "next"; if you're in the reflection, that part is flattery. Glance from a window, where you can't be.
- **Roderick**, by omission, to spare you. *Tell:* hands to his ears.
- **The valet**, by copying: messages pass through him and come back tidied. *Tell:* too-neat copperplate; the original lies in the tarn, mirror-written.
- **The physician** never lies, but writes Latin; **the servant** never lies about time ("some weeks, sir"); **the scores** count acts, not meanings.

**Two witnesses.** A fact two *differently lying* witnesses agree on is **sworn** (wax seal); the house can't edit it. Surviving two distortions, it's true: fair, and learnable by Day 2.

**Red now means a second hand:** someone correcting someone. It proves disagreement, not truth. The hands differ: Roderick's wet slant, the valet's copperplate, the tarn's mirror-writing, later Madeline's scratches. Never "what's red?" but "whose?"

## 5. The mystery
**The Ushers bury one twin a generation, alive, and summon a friend to write it down as a death. The house keeps what the account says.** That's the "direct line" [u04].
- **Day 1:** the P.S.: "Everyone does."
- **Day 2:** paired portraits, one face painted out; both in the tarn.
- **Day 3:** in the library [u27], bound *Accounts of My Sojourn* in other hands, each opening with your first line.
- **Day 4:** many tressels; every old lid has three screws.
- **Day 5:** the physician's note, via the valet: "She is dead. Write nothing else." The tarn: "She is *not* dead."
- **Day 6:** Roderick: "I sent for you because you never hear anything. And you write so beautifully."

## 6. Characters
- **The guest** goes from explainer to author, gaining an excuse a day.
- **Roderick** is your reader, with two hidden pulls: **Cheer** (excuses soothe him; the waltz brightens) and **Candour** (true lines open him, so "I heard her" comes on Day 4, not 6). You can't max both. He edits you: "Strike 'creaked'. Too loud."
- **Madeline** never speaks; she writes. In Act II she's the hero and the red pen.
- **The physician:** honest, barred, unreadable. Trusting him is Act I's hardest call.
- **The valet** is the house's hands: catch him rewriting your slips on night three, and "Quite so." has an author.

## 7. The seven chapters, grown up
Learn, complicate, turn; the turn is a choice you write down.
- **Tarn:** the house cheats twice, then offers its version as a slip. Choose which five to write.
- **Door:** an honest puzzle, still until you move; then façade and water disagree. Finish the wall, or her road.
- **Studio:** hushing taps raises Cheer; unhushed pests cost composure (idle, and he ends under the drapes). The KNOCK: hush it, or let him hear.
- **Burial:** the physician asks to look; let him (his Latin slip) or not. The feet. Four screws, or three.
- **Nights:** set two slips aside, stay up, catch the valet, pocket the originals.
- **Trist:** after the page turns, missed beats cost stairs; she can fall back. The threshold gets a cue.
- **Flight:** three crossings: the tap, your unsworn lines as words, then her.

## 8. The mirror: *A Burial of Some Weeks*
Your Account becomes her level. In the upside-down house, "she died" is a screwed lid, "the draperies" a curtain, an omission an open door, a sworn line a lit passage. Your recorded play is the opponent.
1. **Coffin:** the door puzzle from inside the lid; a missing screw is a gap of light.
2. **Floor:** your hushes fall at your old timings; she taps where you never looked.
3. **Night:** she composes noises to beat your excuse cards.
4. **Stair:** she climbs to the rhythm of your reading.
5. **Door:** she corrects your Account in scratches.

It reveals she heard every supper reading through the floor (your bed is over the vault [u29]), that she wrote the P.S., and that every guest looked and wrote the lie.

## 9. Endings
1. **Considerable.** "She died", sworn, her red struck: the house stands both ways up; your volume joins the shelf.
2. **The Fall.** The truth only on Day 6 or later: she falls upon her brother.
3. **She took my horse.** The truth by Day 4, a screw left out: she rides off; Roderick sinks with the house.
4. **Say, rather.** Every page sworn, all her red standing: nothing to keep, no house in the tarn. *A Sojourn of Some Weeks*, in her hand, about you.

## 10. Music and sound
Roderick's speaking guitar: a plucked string synthesized in code. At supper he plays your page to Von Weber's "last waltz": sworn lines in tune, excuses muffled (*plink.*), omissions as rests. High Cheer sweetens it, so the guitar flatters too (connoisseurs: the waltz is Reissiger's). The tarn plays each bar a beat early. "The Haunted Palace" gets a stanza each evening, and "smile no more" lands in the flight. Studio and nights play in silence. Every cue has a visual twin.

## 11. Build plan
- **Reuse:** the games, each given a third act; the nights' slips and rewriting as the Account engine; the tarn's cheat as the overnight edit; the last card's flip as the mirror.
- **New:** pocket, page, Cheer and Candour, save, `api.record`/`api.mirror`, audio.
- **First milestone (a day):** Day 1, end to end. The tarn; a walk pocketing four slips; a three-blank page; Roderick reading it; one unsworn line edited overnight; a morning glance at the fresh cut and the flattery. Twelve minutes of the loop.

## 12. The risk
It could become "reading blocks of text and making decisions", which Lukas rejected. Keep the page a toy: three blanks, slips dragged, 90 seconds. If the page outlasts the walk, cut a blank.

## 13. A sample moment
Day two, the gallery. Two portraits: a boy, and a girl with her face painted out. In the tarn both faces are there, and they're the same face.

You write: *The family portraits: one to a generation.*

At supper Roderick reads it aloud. "One to a generation. How well you put it."

Overnight the house keeps your word. In the morning each frame holds one portrait; the nail where the second hung is fresh-cut. From the brink, the water shows two faces, and a third, smaller, in a top hat.

You're in the reflection, so it's flattery. Or a warning. You look again from the gallery window, where you can't be in the picture. The third frame is still there, empty.

*One to a generation* is now, technically, true.
