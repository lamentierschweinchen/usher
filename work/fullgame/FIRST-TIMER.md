# First-timer's review of the bible

*A player who hasn't read Poe, 27 October 2026. I read BIBLE.md and played the current build into the door.*

## Where I'd be lost

- **0:15.** "Pale hand" and "copperplate" mean nothing yet; I haven't met them. *Tarn* is a guess, so keep the build's THE TARN label.
- **2:30–6:30.** The tarn has nine rules in four minutes. In play, tapping the water's fallen tree didn't count (only the tree above did), and then a found dot became a squiggle and the smoke vanished. **I thought it was a bug.** I never saw a rim.
- **6:30.** I can't fill the blank: I have no names for the hands. It teaches me to wait, not to look.
- **Interludes.** *Catalepsy* is the fairest clue in the game, and I wouldn't know the word means a trance that passes for death.
- **The door.** In ninety seconds I couldn't find the verb. Tapping and dragging tiles, the gap and the white figure did nothing, except one unexplained red line.
- **Before IV.** When does Madeline die? I'd be carrying a coffin thinking, "wait, she's dead?"
- **IV.** Roderick says lids are never screwed, then blesses the fourth screw. I'd put in four because that's what coffins get, and I wouldn't know it was a choice.
- **Twins.** Nothing says they're twins before the gallery. "Whose turn" needs three rules at once (each house shows its twin, the water runs early, wet paint). I'd miss it.

## Where I'd be bored

- In act one, about twelve interludes, suppers and deep rooms use the same verbs. By the fourth, pinning and excusing is a chore.
- The Account writing itself for twenty seconds every chapter, plus three suppers read aloud, is reading I didn't choose.
- VI is homework if I have to read Ethelred, and great if the early echoes carry it.
- In act two, the tarn and the door below risk feeling like "watch your replay."

## Where I'd be delighted

- The *Turn back* button the horse ignores. "Then how do they get out?"
- *Say, rather: 5.* *R.U., naturally.*, struck in red. *Not wrong. Early.*
- **"You've got the heavy end?" "I thought you had."** That's the one I'd tell a friend.
- Backing out my own screws, one per heartbeat.
- "The rushing gu—"; "That's only the wind." It is.
- The shelf of Accounts that all open with my first line.
- In the build: *Five of five. The house thanks you.*

## The deceit

**I'd catch:** the vanity, the Account's names (*naturally*), the physician's lighting, and Roderick's omissions, if his hands visibly reach his ears.

**I'd miss and resent:**
- *The cheat was hers.* Unless the rim is unmissable once, act two retcons what I took for a bug.
- *The inverted façade.* It's taught and used against me in the same chapter.
- *No lace.* It's too fine a tell unless someone points at lace once.
- *Whose turn.* A comfort player never hears "It was my turn," and no act-two reveal says it. "Did she go down for him?" then asks about a premise I never learned, which breaks your rule 4.

## Act two

Yes, if act one doesn't look finished. At 58 minutes on a weeknight, Poe's fall, told whole, is when I'd close the laptop. The hinge has to interrupt, not wait.

"That's me" lands hard at the screws and at my old excuses answering her knock. If idle defaults wrote my Account, it becomes "that's him." That's fine, but only if I can see which lines were mine.

## The five changes

1. **Spread the tarn's rules over minutes 2–12.** Move the red window and the blank to the first interlude. When the smoke un-finds, pulse her rim once and have the guest mutter "I'd have sworn."
2. **Give everyone the premise.** Put Madeline's "death" on screen before IV, with the physician saying *catalepsy*. Say "twins" by the gallery. Have one act-two reveal state, in her hand, that it was his turn.
3. **Make the screws a visible choice.** On "Thus, thus," Roderick's hands go to his ears, and the physician at the vault door repeats "Before anyone does anything."
4. **Cut the act-one interstitials by a third.** Keep one supper ("Strike 'creaked'") and two deep rooms. A tap finishes the Account.
5. **Make the hinge undeniable, and show the endings.** Strike the last Account line in red before the curtain. After the ending, show five Obra Dinn-style slots and mark which lines I wrote and which I idled.
