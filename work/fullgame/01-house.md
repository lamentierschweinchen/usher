# The Direct Line

## 1. Name and pitch
**The Direct Line.** Between set pieces, search the house and its reflection to learn why the Ushers are always twins and only one stays up. Then play the reflection, as the one who went under.

## 2. The shape (88 min)
Each chapter: house survey (3–4 min), set piece (5–7), Account page.
- Prologue: the letter (2).
- **Act I, *A Sojourn of Some Weeks*** (68): tarn 8, door 9, studio 10, burial 11, nights 11, trist 9, flight 8, Account 2.
- **Act II, *A Burial of Some Weeks*** (16). Coda (2).

## 3. Between chapters: look down, write it down
- **0:00.** Overnight the cutaway changed in three places; one is the house tidying (§4).
- **0:40.** Tap a lit room for a close-up: portrait pairs, the *Vigiliae* [u27], Roderick's paintings [u18], saddles, the vault wall. Objects yield *particulars*: words, dates, profiles.
- **1:30.** Drag the waterline down: the same room in the tarn, with other particulars.
- **2:30.** One exchange: *cheer* or *press*.
- **3:00.** The Account: register pages with blanks (*Of the twins of 1775, ___ went down*). Leave a blank ten seconds and the guest fills it, sensibly and wrongly. Three right and Roderick's guitar resolves. Wrong earns silence. Nothing is gated.

## 4. Truth and deceit
**Red means contested:** two witnesses disagree here, and red never says who's right. The fissure: the house says sound, the water cracked. The moon: Poe's storm had none [u35].
- **The guest** explains. Tell: speed, and *merely*, *naturally*, *no doubt*.
- **The house** tidies while you look down. Tell: a repair has no lace (nothing old here is that clean [u06]) and leaves wax, whose we never say.
- **The tarn** leans true, but it *inverts* (sides, text, roles), *ripples* (in wind it shows another moment: spreading rings, to come; closing, past) and *flatters* (carrying her down shows as carrying her up). The valet is never in it.
- **Roderick** speaks false and sings true.
- **Madeline** only knocks, and the studio pays you to hush her.
- **The physician:** true facts, unstated motives.
- **The scores:** true counts, flattering labels. *Taps hushed* counts times she was ignored.

Set pieces teach each rule before the house uses it.

## 5. The mystery
The house stands on one twin of each generation lying beneath it. At "the fortnight" [u28] one is laid alive in the copper vault (catalepsy is the alibi), and a guest carries and explains. Whoever goes down is painted out. The "direct line" [u04] is bookkeeping.
- **I–II.** Paired portraits, one face scrubbed in each; one birth registered per generation.
- **III–IV.** Vault-wall tallies: fourteen per generation, but one set stops at twelve. In the water, 1775's scrubbed face is the physician's: "the physician of the family" [u07] is family.
- **V–VI.** Roderick's lit tunnel [u18] is this vault, from inside; the 1809 pair has *his* face scrubbed. He went down at twelve; his uncle got him out ("I doubted to whom I spoke" [u09]). The house has crumbled since; now it wants her. The letter's three hands: Roderick's *dying*, Madeline's *quiet* (listen), copperplate's *resting*.
- **Act II.** Every generation had a guest.

**Reveal:** in the nights, Roderick confesses if he trusts you ("I know the sound. I made it."). Otherwise Poe's door [u48].

## 6. Characters
- **The guest:** his auto-fill is the verb you learn to overrule.
- **Roderick:** cheering him and keeping his custom raise trust; pressing and fetching the physician lower it. It shows in his pose, his playing, and whether he visits in V. The trap: friendship rewards complicity, but only his songs show what to break.
- **Madeline:** cargo, then author, then player.
- **The physician:** everyone's suspect. He's been down, and admits it once.
- **The valet:** the house's hand. He tidies, keeps the register and never speaks. Is he the house?

## 7. The seven chapters, grown up
Idling only lets the house tidy, at your cost. No mercy rules; failure costs a particular, a stone or trust. Every chapter ends on a visible fork.
- **Tarn.** Five differences; the house undoes one, betrayed only by its missing lace. Count the lit window, and the gallery opens.
- **Door.** Two cracks; the water swaps sides, so the true branch looks wrong. The shortcut leaves a stone askew and the library shut. Idle 20 seconds and the house moves a stone wrong.
- **Studio.** Roderick talks while you hush, and sings only in silence. KNOCK: "Spare me everything?" Hush it, or let it reach him and hear one true sentence.
- **Burial.** Keep the lure and the feet. Let the physician look and he slips you a casebook page, while Roderick watches. In the vault, match the painting to the archway; count the tallies. Roderick counts your screws.
- **Nights.** Keep "Quite so." Stamps write the Account, and persuaded slips become entries the house defends. Then Roderick's lamp: if he trusts you, he confesses, and you go down.
- **Trist.** Keep the page turn. Missed beats become scratches, the only words she'll have. At the threshold, mace raised, gust leaning: strike, or let the wind.
- **Flight.** A last survey in the storm, then three runs: what you hushed, your false entries, her. Looking back freezes her and drops the house faster.

## 8. The mirror act
After any ending, tap the red *Burial* in the reflected title: you're Madeline, in the existing `C.returned` house set upright, where the real house is the lying reflection.
- **The drowned house (4).** The scrubbed twins say "It's very restful." Their tallies disagree. Every portrait in the water has a top-hatted guest behind it.
- **Nights (4).** You write the slips: forge a noise no card explains.
- **Burial (3).** Be the feet three beats behind, learning the way up.
- **Trist (3).** Strike on his words.
- **Flight (2).** Move only while he looks back.

Revealed: you're the latest cheerful guest; the water needs something under it, not someone.

## 9. Endings
1. **Perfect Keeping.** Keep the whole custom: she stays down, the house stands, Poe's tale never happens. You sign the register. "Some weeks, then, sir."
2. **The Fall.** Poe's. The default.
3. **She Took the Horse.** Spare her once, and confirm 1809 before the trist. Roderick goes down in her place ("I know the way"); she takes your horse.
4. **The Fragments of the "House of Usher".** Only in Act II, with every page right: drag the title's words into the tarn. Nobody goes under; the water closes over the name, quotation marks and all [u50]. Both titles read *Sojourn*. No red.

## 10. Music and sound
WebAudio, optional, always on screen too.
- **The guitar** (Karplus-Strong) judges: a page's phrase hangs until three entries are right.
- **"The Haunted Palace":** a verse per chapter when he trusts you; each is a clue ("two luminous windows": the twins).
- **Von Weber's "last waltz"** [u17] (really Reissiger's) detunes a step a chapter and plays backwards in Act II.
- **Silence** before every tap.

## 11. Build plan
**Reuse:** cut library, map, reflection, all seven games as cores, `ending.js`'s Account.
**New:** `plate.js` (waterline close-ups with `lace:false` and `wax` tells), `account.js` (blanks, auto-fill, three-right), trust, endings, `audio.js`, save, mirror mode, and six plates.
**First milestone (a day): the gallery.** After the door, a four-minute survey: portrait pairs, the waterline, one tidy, the physician on the stair, one page the guitar resolves.

## 12. The risk
Homework, and liars that feel arbitrary. Test: "What lied to you, and how did you know?" If they can't name a tell, cut surveys to three particulars and re-teach.

## 13. A sample moment
In the gallery, you drag the waterline over the fifth portrait pair. Above: a gentleman, 1775, beside a frame scrubbed black. In the water his frame is empty; the scrubbed one holds a squat, hook-nosed profile. You look up. The scrubbed frame now holds a pleasant landscape, and your pen has written *Merely a landscape.* But its trees have no lace, and there's wax on the frame. You strike your own sentence. On the stair, the physician pretends to look for his hat. You drag his profile into the last blank: *___ went down.* Two rooms away, Roderick's guitar, hung on one note all evening, resolves. The physician, without turning: "Twelve days. I counted."
