# Critique from the mirror (03): the house (01) and the account (02)

All three of us arrived at red as "contested", a flattering tarn and the P.S. as Madeline's; 02 and I also both made your recorded play act two's opponent. Those parts are probably right. What differs is the shape of the evening.

## 01, the house: *The Direct Line*

**Strongest idea.** The missing-lace tell. The house's repairs are cut without lace, because "nothing old here is that clean" [u06]. It's a lie told in the art's own grammar: learnable in the tarn, fair from then on, and free in Shadow Theatre. Close behind: leave a blank for ten seconds and the guest fills it in "sensibly and wrongly".

**Weakest as an evening.** About 68 minutes of surveys and register blanks with dates (1775, 1809, fourteen tallies): Obra Dinn bookkeeping laid over the seven toys. The mirror is 16 optional minutes after an ending, so players leave remembering a coda, not the twist. Its own risk section says it: homework.

**Deceit.** Fair, since each tell is taught before it's used, but more checklist than delight: once you know lace, wax and *merely*, you scan for tells. Some can't be read at play speed (rings spreading for the future, closing for the past); some are for connoisseurs only (the moonless storm) or never explained (the wax "whose we never say").

**Build risk.** The heaviest art bill: six close-up plates, portraits with legible profiles, a register interface, and five mirror fragments.

## 02, the account: *An Account of My Sojourn*

**Strongest idea.** The house keeps what you write: your excuse is true by morning ("One to a generation is now, technically, true"). And red as a *hand*: "whose?", not "what?"

**Weakest as an evening.** A writing desk, seven times over: gather slips, fill blanks, hear them read, see them kept. Lukas rejected "reading blocks of text and making decisions", and three blanks a day is still that; the chapter games shrink into machines that hand out slips. Act II is five fragments in 14 minutes.

**Deceit.** At its best it's the most delightful of the three, the lie that fulfils itself, and the tells (the blur under the stamp, fresh lamp-rimmed cuts, copperplate) are fair. But a "sworn" fact, sealed by two witnesses, turns doubt into a logic puzzle, and the uncertainty Lukas asked for evaporates.

**Build risk.** Dragging words into blanks on a phone, and Roderick reading every page back line by line, across all the combinations: the writing load explodes.

## Bet

I'd build 03's shape, every chapter's turn played from below against your recorded self, with 02's self-keeping excuses and 01's missing-lace tell. It's the only one where the twist is played rather than read, Lukas asked for the mirror, and it reuses the most art; I'd switch to 02 if the one-day mirror studio plays like a rerun.
