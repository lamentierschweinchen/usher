# Blueprint 02 · The Account, v2

## What changed and why
- **The page writes itself:** hesitate and the guest fills a blank, wrongly (from 01). Overruling him is the verb; the page is never homework.
- **Swearing is harder:** house and tarn are one witness (from 03); no line about yourself swears honestly.
- **The mystery is the system:** Madeline is being written.
- **Roderick co-authors:** *stet* or strike his red.
- **The mirror edits**, not reruns, to stay distinct from 03.
- **The milestone:** "His sister is ___."

## 1. Name and pitch
**An Account of My Sojourn.** Whatever you write down, the house keeps, while the guest in you writes it for you, sensibly and wrongly.

## 2. The shape (85 min)
Letter 2. **Act I, *A Sojourn of Some Weeks*:** seven days (morning 2, game 5–7, page 1–2): tarn 8, door 8, studio 9, burial 10, nights 11, trist 10, flight 8 = **64**. Confession 3. **Act II, *A Burial of Some Weeks*:** 14. Last page 2.

## 3. Between chapters: gather, write, be read
- **Morning.** Walk the cutaway; tap above to *see*, the water to *glance*; answer people with an excuse or "Go on." Each pockets a **slip** (what, where, when, whose hand).
- **Evening.** Sentences with blanks ("On the stair I met ___, who ___."): drag in a slip or stamp an excuse. Leave one ten seconds and the guest fills it. Strike him; struck lines show.
- **Supper.** Roderick reads it aloud and reddens one line: *stet*, or let his stand.
- **Night.** The house makes your page true above; the tarn keeps what you left out.

## 4. Truth and deceit
One lie, one tell each:
- **You**, by explanation: the blur shows under the stamp.
- **The guest's hand**, by plausibility: the likeliest phrase, never what you saw. *Merely*, *naturally*.
- **The house**, by keeping: it edits itself to match your page, fresh-cut and lamp-rimmed for a day.
- **The tarn** leans true, but *inverts*, *ripples* (rings show the next moment) and *flatters* whoever looks (03). Where you're in the reflection, read vanity.
- **Roderick**, by omission: hands to his ears.
- **The valet**, by copying: too-neat copperplate; the original is mirror-written in the tarn.
- **The physician** never lies, in Latin; **the servant**, about time; **the scores** count acts, not meanings.

**Sworn.** A line is sworn (wax seal), and the house can't edit it, when the water and a *person* agree; house and tarn are one witness [u26]. The catch: the water flatters you, so no line about yourself swears honestly, though Roderick will happily swear to it. You can still be wrong.

**Red means a second hand.** Never "what's red?", always "whose?"

## 5. The mystery
**Madeline isn't dying; she's being written.** Each generation the Ushers send for a friend whose account records a twin's "gradual wasting away" [u15]; the house keeps it; the twin goes down. The "direct line" [u04] is a document.
- **Day 1:** "My sister is ~~dying~~ ~~quiet~~ resting." Your first blank is that word.
- **Day 2:** paired portraits, one face painted out; both in the tarn.
- **Day 3:** earlier guests' *Accounts* in the library [u27]: the same symptoms, word for word.
- **Day 4:** if you let the physician look, his casebook: "Well until the guest's arrival."
- **Day 5:** the valet copying your page into the register.
- **Day 6:** Roderick: "I sent for you because you never hear anything. And you write so beautifully."

Left open: did your page make her ill, or only make it respectable?

## 6. Characters
- **The guest:** two hands, yours and the plausible one you overrule.
- **Roderick:** reader and co-author. His red standing raises **Cheer** (he eats; the waltz brightens); your *stet* raises **Candour** ("I heard her" comes Day 4, not 6). Not both.
- **Madeline** never speaks; she writes, and in Act II holds the pen.
- **The physician:** his casebook is the one account no guest wrote.

## 7. The seven chapters, grown up
Learn, complicate, turn; each turn becomes a line.
- **Tarn:** five differences; the house cheats twice; the lit window. Count five, or six.
- **Door:** an honest puzzle, still until you move; façade and water want different stones; the door opens for *her*. "Opened by ___."
- **Studio:** four pests; taps score best and soothe him (idle, and he flees under the drapes); the KNOCK, hushed or heard. You name the taps.
- **Burial:** the lure; the physician asks to look, Roderick watching; the feet. "Bearers: ___." Three screws or four.
- **Nights:** "Quite so." Set two slips aside, stay up, and catch the valet copying.
- **Trist:** draw blows; noises come first; the page turns. Missed beats cost stairs.
- **Flight:** three crossings: the tap, your unsworn lines as words, her.

## 8. The mirror: *A Burial of Some Weeks*
You are Madeline in the upside-down house your page built: "she died" a screwed lid, "the draperies" a nailed curtain, an omission an open door, a sworn line a lit passage. She holds the red pen, and earns each strike by disproving a line in a set piece turned inside out, against your recorded play (03's ghost):
1. **Coffin:** the door puzzle from inside the lid.
2. **Floor:** tap past your old hushes.
3. **Night:** forge a noise your cards can't stamp.
4. **Stair:** climb to the rhythm of your reading.
5. **Register:** the valet copies the page at dawn; she has five strikes for seven lies.

Revealed: she heard every supper through the floor [u29]; she wrote the P.S.; every guest looked, then wrote the plausible.

## 9. Endings
1. **Considerable.** "She died" stands: the house stands both ways up; your volume joins the shelf.
2. **The Fall.** She strikes the death, but your stair still says "draperies": she climbs only to her brother's door.
3. **She took my horse.** She strikes the death, and you'd sworn the stair: she leaves by the causeway.
4. **Say, rather.** Every Act I line true, she spends her strikes on Roderick's red. Two hands sign; the house has nothing to keep. Last card: *A Sojourn of Some Weeks*, in her hand, about you.

## 10. Music and sound
Roderick's speaking guitar, a synthesized plucked string, plays your page at supper to Von Weber's "last waltz" (really Reissiger's): sworn lines in tune, excuses muffled (*plink.*), omissions as rests. Cheer sweetens it; the guitar flatters too. The tarn plays each bar a beat early; "The Haunted Palace" gets a stanza an evening. Every cue also shows.

## 11. Build plan and first milestone
- **Reuse:** the nights' slip and stamp code as the page; the tarn's cheat as the overnight edit; the last card's flip; the seven games.
- **New:** auto-fill, *stet* or strike, map states keyed to words, a save, `api.record`, four mirror pieces.
- **First milestone (one day): "His sister is ___."** After today's tarn, a page of three blanks: "I stabled my horse for ___", "Differences found: ___", and "His sister is ___", whose slips are the letter's three words. Wait and the guest fills them. Roderick reads it and reddens a line. Next morning Madeline crosses the studio as written: carried (*dying*), silent (*quiet*), or a shut door and lamp (*resting*); the tarn shows a version you didn't write. Ten minutes: what you write, the house keeps.

## 12. The risk
Reading and deciding. Three blanks, an auto-fill so you're never stuck, every consequence shown in the house, never as text. Test: "What did your page do?" Players should point at the house.

## 13. A sample moment
Day two, the gallery. Two portraits: a boy, and a girl with her face painted out. In the tarn both faces are there: the same face.

You hesitate. The guest's hand fills the blank: *One to a generation, naturally.* You let it stand.

Roderick reads it at supper. "How well you put it."

Overnight the house keeps your word: one portrait, and a fresh-cut nail where the second hung. From the brink the water shows two faces, and a third, smaller, in a top hat.

You're in the reflection, so it's vanity. Or a warning. From the gallery window, where you can't be in the picture, the third frame is still there, empty.

*One to a generation* is now, technically, true. You didn't even write it.
