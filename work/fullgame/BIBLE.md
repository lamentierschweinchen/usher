## 1. The promise and the shape

**The promise.** You come to a strange house to cheer up an old friend, and you're very good at explaining things away. For an evening the house, its reflection, your diary and everyone in it mislead you, each in one consistent way you can learn to see through. Then the house turns over. You become the woman you explained away, playing from under the floor against a recording of your own evening, and what she does changes how it ends. No Poe needed; Poe readers get a second game of recognitions and fond misdirection.

**The shape: 82 minutes.**
- **Act one, *A Sojourn of Some Weeks* (52):** letter and arrival 3, I tarn 5, interlude 2, II door 5, gallery 3, III studio 6, collapse 1, IV burial 7, V nights 6, supper 1.5, VI trist 5, library 2.5, VII flight 4, Account 1.
- **The hinge (1).**
- **Act two, *A Burial of Some Weeks* (28):** tarn 2, door 2, studio 4, burial 5, nights 3, trist 4, flight and ending 5, interludes 3.
- **The ending slots (1).**

Each chapter learns and complicates above, and turns below. Act one is Poe's fall as the guest tells it; act two decides what happened.

## 2. The first twelve minutes, for someone who has never read Poe

- **0:00, the title.** *A Sojourn of Some Weeks* over its reflection, *A **Burial** of Some Weeks*, the one changed word in red.
- **0:15, the letter.** Roderick's frantic hand; two words in hands we haven't met. The P.S. is mirror-written: the water's first trick. The only prompt: *THE TARN would like a word.* Tap the water: *Don't look in the tarn. Everyone does.* You just did.
- **1:00, the causeway.** *Turn back.* The horse walks on as the band writes: *Nevertheless, … a sojourn of some weeks.* **Nevertheless.** becomes the refrain. Never press it, and Madeline finds the unused page below.
- **1:40, the arrival.** "Shall I stable him for some weeks, sir?" "A night or two." A peasant: "Twins, the family. Only ever one about."
- **2:30, I. Tarn.** Five differences; either half counts.
  - **A find un-finds.** The smoke stops, her pale rim pulses once, and the guest mutters "I'd have sworn." Tap again: caught.
  - **Early.** The tree lies fallen only in the water, until the tree above falls: *Not wrong. Early.*
  - **The question.** One portrait in a window above, two in the water.
  - **Vanity.** The sixth difference is you, taller in the water.
- **6:30, the Account.** One line, and a tap finishes it. If you claimed the vanity, the water's red: *Say, rather: 5.* Red means "not so".
- **7:00, the first interlude.**
  - **The window.** In the water, a figure in a lit window looks up: *Not a difference. A trick of the water.* The first blank: *In the water's window: ___.* Drag her in, or wait and the guest writes *a trick of the light, naturally.* Red: *not so.* His tell.
  - **Lace.** A mended stone. The guest: "Odd. Not a curl on it."
- **9:00, the stair.** The valet's candle leads you on. The physician, lit from below: "If the sister dies, send for me. Before anyone does anything."
- **10:00, II. Door.** "The family don't use the door, sir." "Then how do they get out?"

By minute twelve, from play alone: nobody is fully reliable.

## 3. Interludes

**Act one has three interludes** (after I, the gallery after II, the library after VI), **one scene** (her collapse, after III) and **one supper** (after V). IV runs straight into V.

### Above: look, explain or let be (2 minutes; deep rooms 3)

1. **0:00.** The valet's candle marks the room. Two or three things differ between house and water: the water running early, the house tidying (no lace), or her edit (a gauze rim).
2. **0:20.** Pin one in both halves, then choose:
   - **Explain it** with an excuse card, each glossed in a line: *the draperies*, *the storm*, *catalepsy* ("a trance that passes for death"), *family custom*, *merely electrical phenomena*, *coincidence*. Overnight the house keeps it (the drape really hangs there), and Roderick's guitar steadies (comfort).
   - **Leave it be.** It goes on the Account's back page, *Unaccounted for*, and Roderick next says one true thing (candour).
3. **Dither ten seconds** and the guest explains it, wrongly. His lines are in paler ink marked *m.*, so you always see which were yours. Below, your ghost plays them in his grey: that's him, not you.
4. **At most one blank**, filled by dragging something found.

Deep rooms add a waterline you drag over a close-up.

**Her collapse (after III).** Madeline crosses the gallery and falls. The physician kneels: "Catalepsy. A trance that passes for death. It passes." Roderick: "She is dead." He says it twice.

**The supper (after V).** Roderick reads one line and edits you: "Strike 'creaked'. Too loud."

### Below: knock, and be explained (about a minute)

Madeline walks the reflected house, upright and lit. Her map is your back page: each thing left be is an open door; each excuse is a thing in her way (a nailed drape, a set stone, a screw). She knocks; above, your recorded self answers with your old excuse. Knock where it can't reach ("the draperies" can't explain a knock below the floor), and the wall gives: that Account line is struck and rewritten in her red.

## 4. Truth and deceit

**One lie and one tell per witness.**
- **The guest and his Account:** count truly, name falsely. *Tell:* *merely*, *naturally*, *no doubt*, and hurried blanks.
- **The house:** edits what you aren't looking at, and keeps your excuses. *Tell:* no lace; nothing old here is that clean [u06].
- **Madeline's edits:** signals, not lies. *Tell:* a gauze rim.
- **The tarn:** true about everyone but the looker, whom it flatters. It mirrors (text backwards, sides swapped; taught by the P.S., used at the door), and it **runs early**, showing what's about to be as if it already were.
  - *Tell:* if you're in the picture, that part is vanity; check from a window, where you can't be.
- **Roderick:** omits in speech, and sings what he omits. *Tell:* his hands go to his ears.
- **The physician:** never lies, but the house lights him from below, like a villain. *Tell:* in the water he's lit from above, and kind.
- **The valet:** copies, and whatever passes through him comes back tidied. *Tell:* copperplate too neat; the original lies in the tarn.
- **The scores:** true counts under flattering labels.

**One liar taught per chapter:** I the house edits; II the tarn mirrors; III Roderick omits; IV the physician looks guilty; V the evidence agrees; VI the book echoes; VII you. The warm deception: chapter I's cheat was hers. The rim pulsed.

**Red, exactly** (replaces LOOK §9):

> **Red means "not so."** It marks a disagreement with what's written or shown, and never says who's right; the hand says who disagrees. The water's red is rippled and mirror-set, Roderick's a wet slant, Madeline's back-slanted with a gauze edge. The house never writes in red: it edits in silence, without lace. Red appears on the fissure (the house disagreeing with itself), *say, rather* lines, the *tap.* and the KNOCK, the title's changed word, and the blood moon [u50]. One red statement per screen. Never UI, hovers, touch states, eyes, blood or light.

**Fairness:** every lie is consistent, with one tell on screen (never sound-only), taught before it's used. Anything the plot needs is shown by two witnesses who lie differently; house and tarn count as one ("reduplication" [u26]). Every player gets the premise; only motives stay open.

## 5. The mystery

**The secret.** Ushers come in twins, and the house stands only while one of each pair lives below, in its reflection. At "the fortnight" [u28] the chosen one is laid in the vault, lid unscrewed: a fortnight to change their mind, then the way closes. A guest is always invited, to carry and to write it up as a death: the "direct line" [u04]. This time it was Roderick's turn. He sent his sister, and you screwed the lid down, taking her fortnight. She came up anyway, early. With nobody below, the house falls.

**Clues, in order:**
- **Arrival:** "Twins, the family. Only ever one about."
- **I:** two portraits in the water, one above.
- **The gallery:**
  - In each pair, one face is painted out above, and the other is missing in the water: each house shows the twin who lives in it.
  - The previous pair's water face is squat and hook-nosed. Drag it into *___ went down*; the physician on the stair, without turning: "Twelve days. I counted." He came back inside his fortnight, and he's here to see she gets hers.
  - In the newest pair, Madeline's face above is wet black paint; in the water, hers is missing and Roderick's shows. The water runs early.
- **After III:** her collapse, and *catalepsy*.
- **IV:** the old lids have screw holes and no screws. Roderick: "Never screwed. It's in the book: *the yet unscrewed lid*." You: "'Yet' means not yet." Roderick: "Not in this house."
- **V:** with candour of 2 or more, Roderick at the lamp says, "It was my turn. She went instead." Otherwise he keeps it for Poe's door [u48].
- **The library:** the *Vigiliae Mortuorum* [u27], the custom spelled out: *a fortnight, the lid unscrewed, a guest to carry and to write.*

**Act two reveals:**
1. **Tarn:** the cheat was hers.
2. **Door:** *P.S. in the hand of M.U.* She wanted a guest who looks; he, one who never hears.
3. **Burial:** inside the lid, in her hand: *His turn. I went.* The screws were the crime, and the third bearer was her.
4. **Last, before the flight:** every generation had a guest: a top hat behind every reflected portrait, and a shelf of *An Account of My Sojourn* in other hands, each opening with your first line.

**Left open:** she went down for him, but meant to stay, or to make him follow? (Below, she's the looker; her account of herself can't be checked.) And is the valet the house?

## 6. The cast

- **The guest (you):** explainer, author of a death, her obstacle, perhaps the one below. His last excuse is true and useless: "That's only the wind." It is the wind [u49].
- **Roderick.** Overjoyed, then afraid of one thing under the floor, then confessing, early or at the door. **His guitar is the relationship.**
  - **Comfort:** excuses tune it. Act one gets easier, act two harder: your excuses are her walls.
  - **Candour:** each thing left be stops his playing for one true line. At 2 or more he sings, and a stanza of "The Haunted Palace" lights in a window.
  - You can't have both at full.
- **Madeline.**
  - **Above:** she crosses rooms without noticing you; to her, you're the ghost. Her three edits: the smoke in I, a cleaned face in the gallery, a slip restored at the nights.
  - **Below:** she writes in mirror-hand that flips readable when touched, as dry as you: "Five of five. The house thanks you."
- **The physician:** looks the villain, never lies. Let him see her and he hands you a casebook page: *Well until the guest's arrival.* A warm frame-up, and true. Below, her one ally.
- **The valet:** his candle leads you between rooms (replacing today's glove). He wrote *resting*, has no reflection, and serves whoever is upstairs; below, his candle leads your ghost.
- **The servant** never lies about time: "Some weeks, sir."

## 7. The seven chapters

**Rules:** idling never finishes a game; no mercy rules or retries (failure is visible and noted); each act-two turn opens on a reveal and rewrites one Account line.

**I. A Somewhat Childish Experiment (tarn: 5 above, 2 below)**
- **Above:** five differences (crack, smoke, portraits, tree, door); her cheat, with its pulse and "I'd have sworn"; your vanity.
- **Below:** she plays spot-the-difference on you. To her it's one house, *0 differences*, and she stops the smoke so you'll look twice.
- **Trick:** it cheats. **Choice:** claim the vanity.
- **Flags:** `tarnCaught`, `tarnVanity`, `tarnSawHer` (set in the first interlude).
- **Build:** either half counts; portraits replace the shutters; the tree falls; the rim pulses; the red window moves to the interlude.

**II. A Mere Different Arrangement (door: 5 above, 2 below)**
- **Above:** drag her, the gap, along the crack; still until you move. On a second façade the water swaps sides, so the true branch looks wrong. Idle 20 seconds and the house sets a stone wrong. The last façade: a whole house, or her shorter route.
- **Below:** your recorded drags slide stones into her way.
- **Trick:** it steers you. **Choice:** long or short.
- **Flags:** `doorRoute`, `stonesAskew`, `doorHouseMoves`.
- **Build, first:** today's door hides its verb (a first-timer found nothing in 90 seconds). Pulse the gap, mime one drag, and let a tap beside the gap move her. Then the second façade and idle cost.

**III. The Cheerfulness of My Society (studio: 6 above, 4 below)**
- **Above:** as built. Roderick sings only in silence, so a spared tap buys a stanza. Before the KNOCK: "You'd tell me if you heard anything?"
- **Below:** milestone 1.
- **Trick:** it rewards the wrong thing. **Choice:** hush the KNOCK, or not.
- **Flags:** `tapsHushed`, `tapsSpared`, `knockSpared`, `mirrorStudio`.

**IV. A Harmless Precaution (burial: 7 above, 5 below)**
- **Above:**
  - Lure the physician with your footsteps.
  - The steps behind you close to none, and a third pair of feet lifts the coffin: "You've got the heavy end?" "I thought you had."
  - Let the physician look, or don't.
- **The screws, a visible choice.** After the third, the screwdriver stops. In the vault door the physician: "Before anyone does anything." Roderick, hands at his ears: "Thus, thus, and not otherwise" [u12]. Turn the fourth, or walk out.
- **Below:** carried in the coffin. Push to bear yourself, or kick to bring the physician. Then *His turn. I went.*, and back out each screw you turned, one per heartbeat.
- **Trick:** it follows you. **Choice:** let him look; three screws or four.
- **Flags:** `burialScrewOut`, `burialGlimpses`, `physicianLooked`.

**V. The Seventh or Eighth Night (nights: 6 above, 3 below)**
- **Above:** kept whole; your cards are the excuses you played. New: stay up past a slip to catch the valet's copperplate rewriting it. Then Roderick's lamp.
- **Below:** Papers, Please from the smuggler's side: she forges noises your cards can't stamp, while the cards wear from *coincidence*, to *most extraordinary coincidence*, to blank.
- **Trick:** the evidence agrees. **Choice:** what goes unaccounted for.
- **Flags:** `nightsUnexplained`, `nightsStair`, `nightsAgreed`, `valetCaught`.

**VI. One of Your Favorite Romances (trist: 5 above, 4 below)**
- **Above:** draw the blows; six-word captions, and the early echoes carry it. MIGHT rises with each flagon [u39]. You may close the book ("Go on."). The page doesn't turn: shield won, Roderick facing the door.
- **Below:** the page turns.
  - Her climb on the heartbeat, with your strokes on her lid. Missed beats cost stairs.
  - The threshold: strike, or let the gust. The last excuse files "the rushing gu—" and stops.
- **Trick:** it swaps who you are. **Choice:** strike or gust.
- **Flags:** `tristLastBlow`, `tristBlows`, `tristClosed`.
- **Build:** split today's game at the page turn.

**VII. A Thousand Waters (flight: 4 above, 5 below)**
- **Above:** three runs: what you hushed, your excuses as words, then her. Looking back freezes them and drops the house faster. Caught, the servant fishes you out: *Fell in: once. Say, rather: the tarn gave you back.*
- **Below:** she moves only while your recorded self looks back. The house falls upward. Then she chooses: his door, or the causeway.
- **Trick:** it remembers. **Choice:** whether to look back.
- **Flags:** `flight` `{looks, fell, madeline}`.

## 8. The hinge and the endings

**The hinge.** The Account's last line writes itself: *Cheerfulness of my society: considerable.* Before you can tap, a back-slanted red hand strikes it. The curtain drops, the stage turns by itself, and **Nevertheless.** appears only on the turned side.

**Breaks:** act-one lines her red rewrote (one per chapter at most). **Openings:** breaks plus things left be. The first listed ending that qualifies wins.

1. ***A Burial of Some Weeks*** (secret). You looked down in every interlude and left the fourth screw out. Your recorded glance meets her eye, and the guest goes below instead. *Guests: one. Say, rather: some weeks.*
2. **The Swap.** Roderick heard her: candour of 2 or more, or her studio KNOCK heard. He goes down himself. The house stands both ways up, and you ride off none the wiser.
3. **She Took the Horse.** Three or more openings, and she lets the gust. She goes past his door to the causeway; he and the house go under. "It's been some weeks, ma'am."
4. **The Fall** (Poe's, the default). She reaches his door. Nobody is below, and the house goes down.
5. **Considerable.** One break or none. She never got past you and stays below; the house stands. You sign the register, and your volume joins the shelf. "Some weeks, then, sir."

**Every last card:** drag the title's words into the tarn, which closes over "House of Usher", quotation marks and all [u50].

**The slots.** Five ending slots, Obra Dinn-style: yours filled, the others hinted ("Someone heard her."), beside your Account with his idle lines marked *m.* **Nevertheless.** returns to the letter, its P.S. upright, in her hand.

## 10. The literary layer

- **The letter:** *Son cœur est un luth suspendu* [u01], glossed in the water: "His heart is a lute; touch it and it sounds." The water's version reads *Mon cœur*: whose heart?
- **The causeway:** "Nevertheless … some weeks" [u03], as a refused button.
- **I:** "a somewhat childish experiment" [u05].
- **II:** "The family don't use the door." Act two answers.
- **III:** Reissiger under Weber's name; the easel's lit tunnel [u18], the vault from inside a coffin; "I dared not speak" [u48]; the lady crossing "without having noticed my presence" [u14] while Madeline is below.
- **IV:**
  - "We two alone bore it" [u29], with three bearers.
  - *The yet unscrewed lid* [u30], misread on purpose and flagged by "Not in this house": the knower's objection ("but they screw it down!") names the crime.
- **V:** *merely electrical phenomena* [u36] as a card.
- **VI:** the echoes run backwards [u40–47]; the gust is real [u49].
- **VII:**
  - a starless storm [u35], its one star Poe's footnote asterisk [u51];
  - "many minutes, many hours, many days" [u48], with your actual playtime's word underlined.
