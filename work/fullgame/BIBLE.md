# A Sojourn of Some Weeks: the bible

*Lead designer, 27 October 2026. This overrides earlier documents. MILESTONE-1.md stands.*

## 1. The promise and the shape

**The promise.** You come to a strange house to cheer up an old friend, and you're very good at explaining things away. For an evening the house, its reflection, your own diary and everyone in it mislead you, each in one consistent way you can learn to see through. Then the house turns over. You become the woman you explained away, playing from under the floor against a recording of your own evening, and what she does changes how the evening ends. No Poe needed; Poe readers get a second game of recognitions and fond misdirection.

**The shape: 87 minutes.**
- **Act one, *A Sojourn of Some Weeks* (58):** letter and arrival 3, I tarn 5, interlude 2, II door 5, gallery 3, III studio 6, supper 2, IV burial 7, vault 3, V nights 6, supper 2, VI trist 5, library and supper 3, VII flight 4, Account 2.
- **The hinge (1):** **Nevertheless.** turns the stage over.
- **Act two, *A Burial of Some Weeks* (28):** tarn 2, door 2, studio 4, burial 5, nights 3, trist 4, flight and ending 5, interludes 3.

Each chapter learns and complicates above, and turns below. Act one ends whole, as the guest tells it (Poe's fall). Act two decides what really happened.

## 2. The first ten minutes, for someone who has never read Poe

- **0:00, the title.** *A Sojourn of Some Weeks* hangs over its reflection, *A **Burial** of Some Weeks*: one word differs, in red.
- **0:15, the letter.** It's in three hands: Roderick's slant, a pale hand for ~~*quiet*~~, the valet's copperplate for *resting*. The P.S. is mirror-written. The only prompt: *The tarn would like a word.* Tap the water and it reads: *Don't look in the tarn. Everyone does.* You just did. That's the first joke, and it teaches looking down.
- **1:00, the causeway.** A button: *Turn back.* Press it and the horse walks on as the band writes: *Nevertheless, … a sojourn of some weeks.* **Nevertheless.** becomes the refrain: the last card's button, and Roderick's word for overruling you. If you never press it, Madeline finds the unused page below.
- **1:40, the arrival.** "Shall I stable him for some weeks, sir?" "A night or two."
- **2:30, I. Tarn.** Spot five differences.
  - **A find can be taken back** while you look away. The cheat has a faint pale rim, which pays off in act two.
  - **Why two portraits?** One lit window shows one portrait above and two in the water: the first-timer's question, at minute four.
  - **The water runs early.** The tree stands above and lies fallen below; at the chapter's end, the tree above falls. *Not wrong. Early.*
- **5:00, vanity.** A sixth difference: you, taller and braver in the water. Claim it if you like.
- **5:40, the red window.** Tap her and your circle is struck out: *Not a difference. A trick of the water.*
- **6:30, the Account** writes itself for twenty seconds.
  - **Red is taught:** if you claimed the vanity, the water's red corrects it: *Say, rather: 5.* Red means "not so".
  - **The first blank:** *P.S. in the hand of ___.* Wait ten seconds and the guest fills it himself: *R.U., naturally.* Red: *not so.* That's his tell, and your diary lies.
- **7:30, the stair.** The valet's candle leads you to the door. The physician, lit from below, passes: "If the sister dies, send for me. Before anyone does anything." You pick a reply.
- **8:30, II. Door.** "The family don't use the door, sir." "Then how do they get out?"

By minute ten, from play alone: the house edits, the tarn runs early and flatters, the Account lies, nobody is fully reliable.

## 3. Interludes

### Above: wake, look, explain or let be (2 minutes; deep rooms 3–4)

1. **0:00.** The valet's candle stands in the room to search. Two or three things differ between the house and the water. Each is the water running early, the house tidying (a repair without lace), or her edit (a gauze rim; three in act one).
2. **0:20.** Pin one in both halves, then choose:
   - **Explain it.** Play an excuse card (*the draperies*, *the storm*, *catalepsy*, *family custom*, *merely electrical phenomena*, *coincidence*) to whoever is near. Overnight the house keeps it: the drape really hangs there, both halves agree, and Roderick's guitar steadies (comfort).
   - **Leave it be.** It goes on the Account's back page, *Unaccounted for*, and at your next meeting Roderick stops playing and says one true thing (candour).
3. **Dither ten seconds** and the guest explains it, sensibly and wrongly. Idle never finishes anything; it writes his default, which your ghost later plays.
4. **1:30.** At most one blank, filled by dragging something you found into an Account line.

**Deep rooms** are searches with a waterline you drag over a close-up: the gallery after II, the vault after IV, the library before VII.

**Suppers** follow III, V and VI: Roderick reads one Account line aloud and answers it. Once he edits you: "Strike 'creaked'. Too loud."

### Below: knock, and be explained (about a minute)

Madeline walks the reflected house, upright and lit. Her map is your back page: each thing you left be is an open door; each excuse is a thing in her way (a nailed drape, a set stone, a screw). She knocks, and in the water above your recorded self answers with your old excuse. Knock where the excuse can't reach ("the draperies" can't explain a knock below the floor), and the wall gives: that Account line is struck and rewritten in her red.

## 4. Truth and deceit

**One lie and one tell per witness.**
- **The guest and his Account:** count truly, name falsely. *Tell:* *merely*, *naturally*, *no doubt*, and hurried blanks.
- **The house:** edits what you aren't looking at, and keeps your excuses. *Tell:* no lace. Nothing old here is that clean [u06].
- **Madeline's edits:** off-gaze too, but signals, not lies. *Tell:* a gauze rim.
- **The tarn:** true about everyone but the looker, whom it flatters. It inverts left and right, and it **runs early**, showing what's about to be as if it already were.
  - *Tell:* if you're in the picture, that part is vanity; check from a window, where you can't be.
  - It's the one witness you can prove wrong, by changing what happens.
- **Roderick:** omits in speech, and sings what he omits. *Tell:* his hands go to his ears.
- **The physician:** never lies, but the house lights him from below, like a villain. *Tell:* in the water he's lit from above, and kind.
- **The valet:** copies, and whatever passes through him comes back tidied. *Tell:* copperplate too neat; the original lies in the tarn, mirror-written. He has no reflection.
- **The scores:** true counts under flattering labels.

**Taught in order, one liar per chapter:** I the house edits; II the tarn inverts; III Roderick omits; IV the physician looks guilty; V the evidence agrees; VI the book echoes; VII you. The warm deception: chapter I teaches "the house edits" with an edit that was hers. The rim was there.

**Red, exactly** (replaces LOOK §9):

> **Red means "not so."** It marks a disagreement: someone, or something, contradicting what is written or shown. It never says who's right; the hand says who disagrees. The water's red is rippled and mirror-set, Roderick's a wet slant, Madeline's back-slanted with a gauze edge. The house never writes in red: it edits in silence, without lace. Red appears on the fissure (the house disagreeing with itself), *say, rather* lines, the *tap.* and the KNOCK (noises someone insists aren't there), the title's changed word, and the blood moon [u50]. One red statement per screen. Never UI, hovers, touch states, eyes, blood or light.

**Fairness:**
1. Every lie is consistent and has one tell on screen, never one you can only hear.
2. Every liar is taught before it's used.
3. Anything the plot needs is shown by two witnesses who lie differently; house and tarn count as one ("reduplication" [u26]).
4. Nothing that matters is for connoisseurs only; what's left open is motive, never mechanism.

## 5. The mystery

**The secret.** Ushers come in twins, and the house stands only while one of each pair lives below, in its reflection. At "the fortnight" [u28] the chosen one is laid in the vault with the lid unscrewed: a fortnight to change their mind, then the way closes. A guest is always invited, to carry and to write it up as a death: the "direct line" [u04]. This time it was Roderick's turn. He sent his sister instead, and you screwed the lid down, which took away her fortnight. She came up anyway, early. With both twins above and nobody below, the house falls.

**Clues, in order:**
- **The letter:** three hands; the mirrored P.S.; the blank *P.S. in the hand of ___*.
- **I:** two portraits in the water, one above.
- **II:** "Then how do they get out?"
- **The gallery:**
  - In each pair, one face is painted out above, and the other is missing in the water: each house shows the twin who lives in it.
  - The previous pair's water face is squat and hook-nosed. Drag it into *___ went down*; the physician on the stair, without turning: "Twelve days. I counted." He came back up inside his fortnight, and he's here to see she gets hers.
  - In the newest pair, Madeline's face above is wet black paint; in the water, hers is missing and Roderick's shows. The water runs early.
- **IV:** the old lids have screw holes and no screws. Roderick: "Never screwed. It's in the book: *the yet unscrewed lid*." You: "'Yet' means not yet." Roderick: "Not in this house."
- **The vault:** one wall's tally stops at twelve. Roderick's painting of a long lit tunnel [u18] is this vault from inside a coffin: the water showed him his own turn, early.
- **V:** with candour of 2 or more, Roderick at the lamp says, "It was my turn. She went instead." Otherwise he keeps it for Poe's door [u48].
- **The library:** the *Vigiliae Mortuorum* [u27], the custom's handbook.

**Act two reveals:**
1. **Tarn:** the cheat was hers.
2. **Door:** *P.S. in the hand of M.U.* She wanted a guest who looks; he, one who never hears.
3. **Burial:** the screws were the crime, and the third bearer was her.
4. **Nights:** the copperplate serves whoever is upstairs.
5. **Last, before the flight:** every generation had a guest. A top hat stands behind every reflected portrait, and the library shelves *An Account of My Sojourn* in other hands, each opening with your first line.

**Left open:** did Madeline go down for him, or to make him follow? (Her account of herself can't be checked: below, she's the one looking.) And is the valet the house?

## 6. The cast

- **The guest (you).** He goes from explainer, to author of a death, to her obstacle, perhaps to the one below. His last excuse is true and useless: "That's only the wind." The doors open. It is the wind [u49].
- **Roderick.** Overjoyed, then afraid of one thing under the floor, then confessing, early or at the door. **His guitar is the relationship.**
  - **Comfort:** excuses tune it, and the waltz plays sweet. Act one gets easier and act two harder, because your excuses are her walls.
  - **Candour:** each thing left be stops his playing for one true line. At 2 or more he sings, and a stanza of "The Haunted Palace" lights in a window.
  - You can't have both at full.
- **Madeline.**
  - **Above:** she crosses rooms without noticing you; to her, you're the ghost. Her three edits: the smoke in I, a cleaned face in the gallery, a slip restored at the nights.
  - **Below:** she writes in mirror-hand that flips readable when touched, as dry as you: "Five of five. The house thanks you."
- **The physician.** Looks like the villain and never lies. Let him see her and he hands you a casebook page: *Well until the guest's arrival*, a warm frame-up, and true. He's family; below, her one ally.
- **The valet.** Of stealthy step. His candle leads you between rooms, replacing today's glove. He wrote *resting*. He has no reflection, and he serves whoever is upstairs; below, his candle leads your ghost.
- **The servant** never lies about time: "Some weeks, sir."

## 7. The seven chapters

**Rules:** idling never finishes a game; no mercy rules, no retries (failure costs something visible, and the Account notes it); each act-two turn opens on a reveal and rewrites one Account line.

**I. A Somewhat Childish Experiment (tarn: 5 above, 2 below)**
- **Above:** five differences (crack, smoke, portraits, tree, door); her rimmed cheat; your vanity; the red window.
- **Below:** she plays spot-the-difference on you. To her it's one house, *0 differences*, and she stops the smoke so you'll look twice.
- **Trick:** it cheats. **Choice:** claim the vanity; tap her.
- **Flags:** `tarnCaught`, `tarnVanity`, `tarnSawHer`.
- **Build:** portraits replace the shutters; the tree falls; the rim.

**II. A Mere Different Arrangement (door: 5 above, 2 below)**
- **Above:** drag her, the gap, along the crack; still until you move. On a second façade the water swaps sides, so the true branch looks wrong. Idle 20 seconds and the house sets a stone wrong. The last façade: a whole house, or her shorter route. "It opened. You hadn't finished."
- **Below:** your recorded drags slide stones into her way.
- **Trick:** it steers you. **Choice:** long or short.
- **Flags:** `doorRoute`, `stonesAskew`, `doorHouseMoves`.
- **Build:** a second façade, the inversion, the idle cost.

**III. The Cheerfulness of My Society (studio: 6 above, 4 below)**
- **Above:** as built. Roderick sings only in silence, so a spared tap buys a stanza. Before the KNOCK: "You'd tell me if you heard anything?"
- **Below:** milestone 1.
- **Trick:** it rewards the wrong thing. **Choice:** hush the KNOCK, or not.
- **Flags:** `tapsHushed`, `tapsSpared`, `knockSpared`, `mirrorStudio`.

**IV. A Harmless Precaution (burial: 7 above, 5 below)**
- **Above:**
  - Lure the physician with your footsteps (rule shown first).
  - The steps behind you close to none, and a third pair of feet lifts the coffin: "You've got the heavy end?" "I thought you had."
  - Let the physician look, or don't.
  - The old lids. Four screws (Roderick on the last: "Thus, thus, and not otherwise" [u12]), or walk out with three.
- **Below:** carried in the coffin. Push to bear yourself, or kick to bring the physician; then back out each screw you turned, one per heartbeat.
- **Trick:** it follows you. **Choice:** let him look; three screws or four.
- **Flags:** `burialScrewOut`, `burialGlimpses`, `physicianLooked`.

**V. The Seventh or Eighth Night (nights: 6 above, 3 below)**
- **Above:** kept whole; your cards are the excuses you played. New: stay up past a slip to catch the valet's copperplate rewriting it. Then Roderick's lamp.
- **Below:** Papers, Please from the smuggler's side: she forges noises your cards can't stamp, while the cards wear from *coincidence*, to *most extraordinary coincidence*, to blank.
- **Trick:** the evidence agrees. **Choice:** what goes unaccounted for.
- **Flags:** `nightsUnexplained`, `nightsStair`, `nightsAgreed`, `valetCaught`.

**VI. One of Your Favorite Romances (trist: 5 above, 4 below)**
- **Above:** draw the blows; Ethelred's MIGHT rises with each flagon [u39]; the echoes come before your blows. You may close the book ("Go on."). The page doesn't turn: shield won, Roderick facing the door.
- **Below:** the page turns.
  - Her climb on the heartbeat, with your strokes on her lid. Missed beats cost stairs.
  - The threshold: strike, or let the gust. The last excuse files "the rushing gu—" and stops.
- **Trick:** it swaps who you are. **Choice:** strike or gust.
- **Flags:** `tristLastBlow`, `tristBlows`, `tristClosed`.
- **Build:** split today's game at the page turn.

**VII. A Thousand Waters (flight: 4 above, 5 below)**
- **Above:** three runs: what you hushed, your excuses as words, then her. Looking back freezes them and drops the house faster. If the collapse takes you, the servant fishes you out: *Fell in: once. Say, rather: the tarn gave you back.*
- **Below:** she moves only while your recorded self looks back. The house falls upward. Then she chooses: his door, or the causeway.
- **Trick:** it remembers. **Choice:** whether to look back.
- **Flags:** `flight` `{looks, fell, madeline}`.

## 8. Endings

**Breaks:** act-one lines her red rewrote (one per chapter at most). **Openings:** breaks plus things left be. The first listed ending that qualifies wins.

1. ***A Burial of Some Weeks*** (secret). You looked down in every interlude and left the fourth screw out. Your recorded glance meets her eye, and the guest goes below instead. *Guests: one. Say, rather: some weeks.*
2. **The Swap.** Roderick heard her: candour of 2 or more, or her studio KNOCK heard. He goes down himself. The house stands both ways up, and you ride off none the wiser.
3. **She Took the Horse.** Three or more openings, and she lets the gust. She goes past his door to the causeway; he and the house go under. "It's been some weeks, ma'am."
4. **The Fall** (Poe's, the default). She reaches his door. Nobody is below, and the house goes down.
5. **Considerable.** One break or none. She never got past you and stays below; the house stands. You sign the register, and your volume joins the shelf. "Some weeks, then, sir."

**Every last card:** drag the title's words into the tarn, which closes over "House of Usher", quotation marks and all [u50]. **Nevertheless.** returns to the letter, its P.S. now upright, in her hand.

## 9. Music and sound

Optional, on after the first tap, with a visible mute; every cue also shows.
- **Roderick's guitar**, a Karplus–Strong plucked string, follows the contour of his lines. Comfort tunes it; your excuses muffle it to a *plink*.
- **Von Weber's "last waltz"**, in 3/4, is the map theme, drifting a step out of tune each chapter. The sheet music reads *say, rather: Reissiger.* Below, it plays inverted and backwards.
- **The tarn plays a bar early.**
- **"The Haunted Palace":** a stanza a chapter if he trusts you; V and VI at the hinge.
- **Silence** in the nights and before every tap.

## 10. The literary layer

- **The letter:** *Son cœur est un luth suspendu* [u01], glossed in the water: "His heart is a lute; touch it and it sounds." The water's version reads *Mon cœur*: whose heart?
- **The causeway:** "Nevertheless … some weeks" [u03], as a refused button.
- **I:** "a somewhat childish experiment" [u05]; an unaided catch is logged as *scrutinizing observer* [u06].
- **II:** "The family don't use the door." Act two answers.
- **III:** Reissiger under Weber's name; "I dared not speak" [u48]; the lady crossing "without having noticed my presence" [u14] while Madeline is under the floor.
- **IV:**
  - "We two alone bore it" [u29], with three bearers.
  - *The yet unscrewed lid* [u30], misread on purpose and flagged by "Not in this house": the knower's objection ("but they screw it down!") names the crime.
- **The vault:** the tunnel painting [u18]; Holberg's *Subterranean Voyage* [u27] on her shelf.
- **V:** *merely electrical phenomena* [u36] as a card.
- **VI:** the echoes run backwards [u40–47]; the gust is real [u49].
- **VII:**
  - a starless storm [u35], its one star Poe's footnote asterisk [u51];
  - the final Account justified so a river of white runs down the fissure;
  - "many minutes, many hours, many days" [u48], with your actual playtime's word underlined.
- **Throughout:** *some weeks*, resolved by every ending.
