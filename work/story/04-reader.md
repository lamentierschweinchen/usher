# 04 · The reader

## 1. Name and pitch

**I Continued the Story.** You hold the book while a crack runs down the House of Usher along the path of your reading, and the only way to free Madeline from the vault is to read the house down.

## 2. Whose experience

The player is the reader: not the narrator, but whoever holds the page he rides, reads and flees inside.

In Usher, reading does things. The narrator reads the house's face [u02], then reads the "Mad Trist" aloud until it comes true [u40–u47], "as if … there had been found the potency of a spell" [u49]. Roderick's theory of the house is a theory of print: life from "the order of their arrangement", its "long undisturbed endurance" and "its reduplication" [u26]. That is a text: set in 1845, reduplicated by every reader. The fissure zigzags "from the roof … down the wall" into the tarn [u06], as an eye goes down a page. The last words name the book: "the '*House of Usher*'" [u50]. The house that falls is the text.

## 3. The arc

One page. Poe's column is the house; the bottom margin is tarn and vault. The column is never rewritten: touched, it resounds like the epigraph's lute [u01], but your only power is to go on.

1. **The facade** [u01–u06]. You try the narrator's "childish experiment" [u05] and rearrange the particulars [u02]. The masonry snaps back, darker. Then you find the fissure. *Follows Poe.*
2. **The one who crosses** [u03–u15]. Roderick's letter, then his face. One sentence walks across the column and out the margin: Madeline. It can't be selected or kept [u15]. *Follows.*
3. **The palace** [u16–u27]. "The Haunted Palace" [u20–u25] is a head, a house and this page. Stanzas V–VI darken their own type. *Follows, abridged.*
4. **The lid** [u28–u30]. You see "the mockery of a faint blush" [u30]; turning the page screws the lid down. Her lines fold into the bottom margin, "immediately beneath" [u29]. *Departs: the turn is yours.*
5. **Seven or eight days** [u31–u32]. Roderick listens to nothing. Letters in her folded line shift, unremarked. You may stop: the house stands, the bookmark keeps her, the margin knocks until you return. *Departs: Poe's reader can't keep her.*
6. **The Mad Trist** [u36–u48]. You read Sir Launcelot's cheap quest aloud. Each sound waits for your turn, rising from the margin. Twice you do as the narrator did: "I continued the story" [u40]. Then Roderick glosses your reading. *Follows nearly verbatim.*
7. **The tarn** [u49–u51]. The column parts along the fissure like "ebony jaws" [u49], and she stands in the gap. The narrator flees [u50]; the reader stays. The moon shines through your reading path; the tarn closes over the title. What floats is Poe's footnote [u51], held back for the end, its asterisk the only star in a night with "no glimpse of the moon or stars" [u35]. *Departs: the footnote moves.*

## 4. The first minute

Paper and ink. Two French lines hang at the top like a lute; the title is a roofline. The opening paragraph, cut to its bones, stands justified as a wall, with gaps where "the vacant eye-like windows" sit. Beneath, the same paragraph hangs inverted and faint: the tarn.

No instructions. A touched word rings faintly. Four phrases are loose: "the bleak walls", "the vacant eye-like windows", "a few rank sedges" and "a few white trunks". The player reorders them. The tarn shows them "re-modelled and inverted", the column settles back into Poe's order, and the ink darkens a shade [u02].

The next page names "a barely perceptible fissure" [u06]. Scrutinize, and it's there: a thin river of white through the justified type, title to tarn. Each turn, it runs further.

## 5. What the game can say that the tale can't

**You are not watching the House of Usher fall. Your reading is the fall, and holding back keeps her buried.**

The tale stages this but leaves the narrator an alibi: "the coincidence alone" [u40]. Print can only report another man's reading. A page that waits for your turn removes it.

The second half bites. The fall is the ending, so the only way to save the house is to stop. Poe's word for keeping her unfinished is "preserving her corpse for a fortnight" [u28]. Preservation is premature burial. Readers notice the "faint blush" and the "suspiciously lingering smile" [u30], suspect, and read on in silence, as Roderick did: "I dared not speak!" [u48]. The game lets you stop, and stopping costs her.

## 6. What only this tale could do

A reskinned gothic has a haunted house. Usher has a haunted *reading*:

- A poem draws the house [u20–u25].
- A tale within the tale comes true as it's read [u37–u47].
- A theory makes arrangement plus reduplication alive [u26].
- The crack is a line of sight [u06].
- The last sentence names the book [u50].

It even ships a decoy game. The Trist is a quest with a door, a monster, a prize and rules on a shield: "Who slayeth the dragon, the shield he shall win" [u42]. It is "uncouth and unimaginative prolixity" [u37], read aloud over the real event in the cellar.

## 7. The seed

**Keep the crack; discard "read too fast."**

Poe drew the crack as a reader's path [u06]. The speed penalty is wrong three ways:

- It makes the climax a fail state, though the fissure widens as the story ends [u50]. Falling *is* finishing.
- It measures nothing: page time can't tell a slow reader from an absent one, and Lukas's own edition rules out timed reading.
- It misplaces the guilt. The tale's sin is haste in *closing*: "upbraid me for my haste" [u48].

So the crack moves at your pace and reaches the tarn on the last line. The clock survives in reverse, measuring how long you leave her. When Roderick says "many minutes, many hours, many days" [u48], the game underlines whichever is true.

## 8. Why it's small

One page, one text, one verb. About 2,500 words of Poe over some 25 turns, cuts shown as joints in the masonry. Touch matters in four places: the particulars, Madeline's sentence, the margin and the Trist. One ending; the unfinished state is just a bookmark. Assets: type, paper, a hairline, a reflection, about six sounds. Twelve to twenty minutes.

## 9. The risk

**It becomes an e-book with a crack graphic: a progress bar dressed as a fissure, paged through with nothing at stake.** Watch in playtest:

- Do players hesitate before turning during the Trist?
- Do they find the margin's movement unprompted?
- Does Roderick's "Not hear it?" make them feel accused rather than informed?

Rhythmic turns through the Trist mean the causality has failed. The fix is never a timer: every sound waits for a hand.

## 10. A sample moment

*The Trist, third sound. The fissure is two lines from the tarn.*

> … fell down at his feet upon the silver floor, with a mighty great and terrible ringing sound.

You turn the page.

From beneath the column, not beside it: a muffled, metallic ringing. The margin's asterisk is open. The Trist lines you read aloud rise into the margin; beside each, a second hand writes, small and fast:

> the breaking of the hermit's door — *say, rather, the rending of her coffin*\
> the death-cry of the dragon — *the grating of the iron hinges of her prison*\
> the clangor of the shield — *her struggles within the coppered archway of the vault*

Below, set alone, as you'd annotate a reader rather than a text:

> *Not hear it? — yes, I hear it, and have heard it.*

The arrow back is gone.
