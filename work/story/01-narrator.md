# The Cheerfulness of My Society

*Story direction 01 · the narrator's seat*

## 1. Name and one-line pitch

**The Cheerfulness of My Society.** You are the friend Roderick sent for to keep him calm: every sensible explanation you offer works, and keeps his sister in the vault.

## 2. Whose experience

The player is the narrator, the only person in the tale free to choose. Madeline is sealed in; Roderick is "a bounden slave" to terror [u12]; the house is a house. The narrator could leave, object or look twice; each time he does the reasonable thing. Summoned for "the cheerfulness of my society" [u03], he hands the fissure to a hypothetical "scrutinizing observer" [u06] and does "not feel at liberty to dispute" the vault [u28]. He screws down the lid, sleeps directly above her [u29], and blames her sounds on the draperies [u32]. When the romance he reads aloud comes true, it is "the coincidence alone" [u40]. "*We have put her living in the tomb!*" [u48] includes him.

The tale makes him our window; the game makes him our hands. It lives in the gap between what the player notices and what he writes down.

## 3. The arc

**The rule.** In every scene the friend's move, usually Poe's own explaining sentence, is lit and one click away. Attending is anything else: tracing, following, waiting. Explaining calms Roderick, which is the job. Attending unsettles him (Poe's law: awareness of fear "served mainly to accelerate the increase itself" [u05]) and adds a red note to the margin and a red stroke to the drawn fissure. Nothing saves Madeline.

1. **The tarn [u02–u06].** Arrival, the reflection, the hairline. Poe.
2. **The studio [u08–u15].** Madeline crosses the far end of the room [u14]. Follow her to the door, or turn to her brother's tears.
3. **The days [u16–u27].** Painting, guitar, "The Haunted Palace", books, in three short days. The turn: he describes an atmosphere condensing "about the waters and the walls", and the narrator "started" [u26], having imagined exactly that at the tarn [u05]. Poe's line: "Such opinions need no comment, and I will make none." The alternative: *I saw it too.*
4. **The vault [u28–u30].** Consent, the carry, the face, the lid. The player turns the screws; whether they lingered on the blush is the margin's business.
5. **The nights [u31–u32].** Departure: Poe gives one sleepless night, the game four, the last his; Roderick heard her "many, many days ago" [u48]. Each night, a sound beneath the floor, and the draperies to blame.
6. **The reading [u33–u47].** "Merely electrical phenomena" [u36], then the Mad Trist read aloud phrase by phrase. Departure: the player may stop reading. The three sounds come anyway, with no sentence to hide in.
7. **The door [u48–u49].** No choice. "We", "Madman!", Madeline on the threshold. Poe, word for word.
8. **The causeway [u50].** Flee, turn. The moon finishes whatever fissure you left undrawn; the tarn closes over the name. Departure: what remains is your account, the 1845 text unchanged, with your margin. Explain everything and the margin is clean: you are the narrator readers trust.

Events never leave Poe: no rescue, no trip down the stair. The tale is fixed; the margin is yours.

## 4. The first minute

A letter unfolds: a few agitated lines in Roderick's hand, ending on "my only personal friend" (ours; Poe summarises it [u03]). The house at dusk in ink, the tale's first sentence setting beneath it. Touch the walls, the eye-like windows, the sedges: each yields its phrase [u02]. Poe's own experiment, "a mere different arrangement of the particulars of the scene": the player drags sedges and trunks about. It doesn't help. Ride to the brink and the picture inverts into the tarn. In the reflection, a hairline runs from roof to water. Trace it, and a first red stroke marks the house and *I saw it* the margin. Leave it, and the page sets Poe's "scrutinizing observer" [u06]. Nothing has said "choose".

## 5. What the game can say that the tale can't

**The narrator's good sense is what keeps Madeline in the vault.**

In the tale his explanations are our only light, so we take them for sanity in a mad house and finish trusting him. A game can set the unexplained thing beside each explanation; taking it becomes an act, and acts accumulate. Poe records one silence in two temperaments: Roderick's "I dared not speak!" [u48] and the narrator's "presence of mind to avoid exciting, by any observation, the sensitive nervousness of my companion" [u45]. Fear silences one, tact the other. The player does it, then hears Roderick say "We" and call them "Madman!" The explanations genuinely help (Roderick plays again, sleeps, warms to you), so it isn't a sermon. It's being the friend you were summoned to be.

## 6. What only this tale could do

Its climax is a man reading aloud while the book comes true [u38–u47], so the peak verb is reading, Lukas's own subject. Its tomb lies under the reasonable man's bed [u29], so his failure is one of listening, which a game can measure without a monster. And it is made of documents (letter, ballad, reading list, romance, footnote [u51]) and ends on its own title, in quotation marks, sinking [u50]. The house falls; the manuscript survives. A gothic reskin has a monster. This has a witness and his account.

## 7. The seed

**Change it: keep the crack, drop the clock.** The fissure is the tale's spine, "barely perceptible" at the start [u06], blazing at the end [u50]. It measures what the narrator lets himself see, so it grows with attention, not speed. Speed is the wrong failure: the narrator's fault is composure, not haste; he reads on, steadily, three times. A pace penalty makes reading a test (Lukas's own edition refuses timed reading) and the Mad Trist a race, when in Poe it is a summoning. Pace survives once: each passage of the Mad Trist read aloud brings the next sound. Read on and it arrives inside a sentence that explains it; stop, and nothing covers it.

## 8. Why it's small

Eight scenes, each one drawing and one verb (look, follow, listen, carry, screw, lie awake, read, flee), with about a dozen explain-or-attend moments. The text is Poe, abridged; original writing (the letter, the nights, the margin notes) stays under 1,500 words. One value drives Roderick's manner, the crack and the margin. No inventory, map, fail state or branching event. Fifteen to twenty minutes, and everyone reaches the end.

## 9. The risk

**It becomes a morality quiz.** Players spot that attending is righteous, attend to everything, and the narrator becomes a strawman. The explanations must tempt: usually plausible (most noises in a storm are the storm) and visibly kind, as Roderick's eye relights and the guitar returns. Attending must cost the friendship. Watch first-time players: if nearly all attend every time, the explanations are too weak; if none do, the evidence is too faint.

## 10. A sample moment

*The Mad Trist, the first sound [u39–u40].*

> "…he so cracked, and ripped, and tore all asunder, that the noise of the dry and hollow-sounding wood alarummed and reverberated throughout the forest."

You pause. From some very remote portion of the mansion: a cracking, a ripping. Stifled, dull, and exactly that.

Roderick has not moved. The storm works at the sashes.

In your account, a sentence has begun to set itself: *It was, beyond doubt, the coincidence alone —*

Under your finger, the next line of the romance is lit and waiting: **But the good champion Ethelred, now entering within the door…**

Read it, and the sentence finishes itself.

Wait, and the house does not wait with you.
