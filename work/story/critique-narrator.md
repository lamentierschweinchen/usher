# Critique · narrator (01)

## 02 Madeline · *Without the Door*

**Strongest idea.** The reversed causality. Input comes before text, so the player acts first and then a sentence arrives to explain her away. The text backs it exactly: the narrator calls her coffin breaking "the echo" of Sir Launcelot's sound [u40]. Its rule, "every act gets a sentence, even a wrong one", is the best in the room.

**Weakest point.** It gives away the tale's one mystery in the first minute. We know she's alive from her first crossing, so Roderick's confession [u48] confirms instead of landing. Two-thirds of the game is a woman lying still in the dark. The draft names the risk, but holding a key to strain is not a pleasure to play with.

**Misreading.** Only an overclaim. "The only one who acts" ignores the narrator, who carries her down and screws down her coffin lid [u29–u30].

## 03 House · *Perfect Keeping*

**Strongest idea.** Sounds appear as written words rising through a cut-away house, and the player either holds them in the masonry or lets them through to Roderick. It's the most toy-like system here. The arc from a "soundless" day [u02] to "the voice of a thousand waters" [u50] is a real find.

**Weakest point.** The early fall. If the player keeps too much, "the last pages sink unread". So the player who works the system hardest loses the Mad Trist, the best scene in the tale, in a 15-minute game.

**Misreadings.**
- The footnote [u51] backs the sentience of *vegetable* things only. Usher's version, Poe says, "trespassed … upon the kingdom of inorganization" [u26]. Living stone is exactly what the men of science don't support.
- The guest doesn't play. Roderick plays the guitar; the narrator listens [u16].
- The fissure isn't there "at first glance". Poe offers it only to "a scrutinizing observer" [u06].

## 04 Reader · *I Continued the Story*

**Strongest idea.** The page is the house. Justified type is masonry, the fissure is a white river through the column (a real typesetter's flaw), and the tarn is the paragraph turned upside down. Reading Roderick's theory of the stones [u26] as a theory of print is the sharpest reading here.

**Weakest point.** The verb. Turning pages is what the player would do anyway. "Stop, and the bookmark keeps her" only bites for someone who abandons a 15-minute game, and nobody judging a jam will.

**Misreading.** The "childish experiment" [u05] is looking into the tarn, not rearranging things. Poe's "different arrangement of the particulars" is achieved *by* the reflection [u02]. My first draft made the same mistake.

## Bet

I'd back 04 to win the room: the page-as-house is the image a judge will still see tomorrow, and only a tale that ends on its own title [u50] could carry it. It wins only if turning a page costs something or takes some craft during the Mad Trist; without that, it's an e-book with a crack graphic.
