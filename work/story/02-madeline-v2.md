# The Coincidence Alone (seat: Madeline, v2)

## What changed and why

V1 was the room's fourth solemn complicity piece, and stillness was its risk. V2 makes the misreading an opponent. Poe's narrator has an excuse for everything, all verbatim, and Madeline's game is to make a noise he can't explain. The key is his own book: sounds that land on the Mad Trist leave him only "coincidence", and coincidence wears out. The prize is Poe's italic *did* [u49]. Borrowed: the narrator's explanations as engine (01, inverted into the adversary) and the Trist as a decoy game with rules on a shield (04). The cataleptic stretch is gone.

## 1. Name and pitch

**The Coincidence Alone.** You are Madeline, buried alive beneath a man who can explain anything, and you have one stormy night to make a noise he can't.

## 2. Whose experience

Madeline is the only character who acts [u48–u49], and she never speaks. Her vault lies "immediately beneath" the narrator's bed [u29]. Roderick hears her and "dared not speak" [u48]. The narrator speaks, and his sentence for everything is her opponent: catalepsy [u30], madness [u31], the furniture [u32], electricity [u36], fancy, the storm, coincidence [u40], the gust [u49]. She isn't an avenger ("upbraid" is Roderick's fear [u48]); she has a problem to solve.

## 3. The arc

The rule from v1 stands: **every word on screen is Poe's, and none is hers.** Each sound you make arrives overhead as a line of his account. An explained sound files an excuse in a margin ledger; an unexplained one opens the fissure a step.

1. **The lid [u30].** You try to show two men you're alive. The ledger opens.
2. **The nights [u29–u32].** A knock freezes Roderick mid-step, and the narrator files it under "the mere inexplicable vagaries of madness" [u31]. His own dread goes down as "the gloomy furniture of the room" [u32]. *Departure: a short sandbox where Poe has one paragraph.*
3. **The window [u34–u36].** Your loudest effort gets two excuses at once: "merely electrical phenomena not uncommon — or … the rank miasma of the tarn." Then he opens a book: "I will read, and you shall listen."
4. **The Mad Trist [u37–u47].** A sound that lands on the romance's own words stops him mid-sentence. The excuses weaken from "the coincidence alone" [u40] to "this second and most extraordinary coincidence" [u45] to none at all: "Completely unnerved, I leaped to my feet" [u47]. The shield's rule turns out to be yours: "Who entereth herein, a conqueror hath bin" [u42]. *Departure: Poe leaves the synchrony uncanny; here it's her trick.*
5. **The gloss [u48].** Roderick finally speaks, glossing the Trist correctly ("say, rather, the rending of her coffin") and striking through your ledger as you climb.
6. **The door [u49].** One excuse is left, and Poe supplies it. Then *did*. You fall inward, and he dies "a victim to the terrors he had anticipated." *Exactly Poe.*
7. **The tarn [u50].** The moon shines through the fissure you opened, and the ledger sinks with "the House of Usher."

## 4. The first minute

Torchlight; a lid turned aside; two faces above you, one of them your own [u30]. Holding brings colour to your face; a tap curves your lip. You are trying to tell them. The account answers: "the mockery of a faint blush upon the bosom and the face, and that suspiciously lingering smile upon the lip which is so terrible in death." The margin files its first excuse: "as usual in all maladies of a strictly cataleptical character." The screws turn, one for each word of "We replaced and screwed down the lid". Dark. Overhead, a thin line of light: his room.

## 5. What the game can say that the tale can't

**The Mad Trist scene is not a coincidence but a contest, and Madeline wins it.**

The tale shows one side: excuses weakening from fancy to coincidence to none. Seen from beneath, that is a scoreboard. A game hands the player the other side and lets them find the trick: use his calming book as a script until coincidence breaks. Not a sermon about complicity: the pleasure of out-arguing a man with an answer for everything, with only noise and timing.

## 6. What only this tale could do

Usher's explainer has a short, escalating set of excuses, and it ends on an italic. Its romance comes with an answer key: Roderick glosses the three noises as her three acts [u48]. Poe's other dead women return to mourners; Madeline returns through a reading she has hijacked.

## 7. The seed

**Keep the crack and a clock, but the clock is his.** The fissure [u06] opens only where he fails, until the moon comes through [u50]. The clock is his reading aloud, done to "pass away this terrible night" [u36]. Each Trist passage is a window: land your sound before he reads past its phrase, or it's lost in "the ordinary commingled noises of the still increasing storm" [u40]. "Read too fast" survives as his reading, never yours.

## 8. Why it's small

One screen: his account above, her dark below. One input, hold or tap, with timing. About 1,500 words of Poe, including ten verbatim excuses. No lines for Madeline, one ending, 12–15 minutes.

## 9. The risk

**The aha doesn't land.** If players never realise the Trist is the key, they flail at random and the scene becomes a quick-time event. Watch whether testers sync to the reading unprompted by the second passage. Cure: make the first accidental hit visible. The line stutters ("I started, and for a moment, paused" [u40]), teaching the rule without a tutorial.

## 10. A sample moment (the door)

The ledger fills the margin, every line struck through in Roderick's hand. Above:

> "*Madman! I tell you that she now stands without the door!*"

You hold. The panels swing:

> "It was the work of the rushing gust —"

In the margin a new excuse starts to file itself, letter by letter: *rushing gu*. It stops. The line runs on:

> "but then without those doors there *did* stand the lofty and enshrouded figure of the lady Madeline of Usher."

The half-filed excuse unwrites itself. *Did* sits in the edition's red.

## 11. The slice

Build the Mad Trist through to the door [u36–u49]: 6–8 minutes, ledger pre-filled from the nights. It holds the aha, the escalation and the punchline, in the tale's most famous scene. It needs a text column, one input, three timing windows, a ledger and three sounds. If ten minutes remain, add the lid as a cold open.
