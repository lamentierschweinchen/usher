# Without the Door (seat: Madeline)

## 1. Name and one-line pitch

**Without the Door.** You are the lady Madeline, the only person in Usher who acts and the only one who never speaks. You play on the far side of every door the narrator shuts, while his sentences overhead get you wrong.

## 2. Whose experience

The player is Madeline. The tale gives her three appearances and no words [u14, u30, u49]; her only sound is "a low moaning cry." Yet she is the only one who acts. Roderick fears [u12] and the narrator explains [u36, u40], while she survives a week in a screwed-down coffin, breaks out, climbs and arrives.

All of it happens offstage, and the tale marks where the stage ends: "a door, at length, closed upon her" [u14]; the lid screwed down, the iron door secured [u30]. The narrator helped carry her down and sleeps directly above her vault [u29]. He hears "low and indefinite sounds" [u32] and blames the storm. Roderick heard "her first feeble movements" and "dared not speak" [u48]. Hers is the untold second story, and in Usher it lies on the storey beneath his bed.

## 3. The arc

One rule: **every word on screen is Poe's, and none of them is hers.** His text is the only light in her dark; you act beneath it, and it answers.

1. **The crossing [u14].** You cross the studio. Whatever you do, the sentence says you did it "without having noticed my presence."
2. **Through the door [u15–u16].** The door closes and the camera stays with you. In the studio they "painted and read together"; "The Haunted Palace" comes through the wall as a song you can't answer. *Departure: we go where the narrator can't.*
3. **The lid [u28–u30].** By torchlight, two faces look down at you, one of them your own. All you can summon is colour and a curve of the lip. They read these as "the mockery of a faint blush" and "that suspiciously lingering smile upon the lip which is so terrible in death." The lid is screwed down.
4. **The vault [u29–u32].** Copper, iron, no light. Each small movement adds a line to the narrator's sleepless night overhead. Roderick sits "as if listening to some imaginary sound" [u31]. He hears you.
5. **The reading [u37–u47].** Above you, the narrator reads the "Mad Trist" aloud. Its three noises are your three acts: the coffin rending, the iron hinges grating, the struggle in the copper archway. Each lands on one of his sentences; the first two he calls coincidence. *Exactly Poe, with the causality reversed.*
6. **The stair [u48].** You climb while Roderick confesses: "*We have put her living in the tomb!*" For the first time, a sentence about you is true. "Is she not hurrying to upbraid me for my haste?" Your pace answers; the game gives her no motive.
7. **The threshold [u49].** The doors open. Your last input is to let go. You fall inward, and the tale's verdict stands: he dies "a victim to the terrors he had anticipated."
8. **The fissure [u50].** From the causeway, the moon shines through the crack you widened; the tarn closes over "the House of Usher."

## 4. The first minute

The epigraph appears on black: *Son cœur est un luth suspendu.* You touch it, and one string sounds. Then the studio [u08]: crimson slits of window, two men, a long dark floor. Holding a key walks you forward at a dying pace. Above the room, the narrator's line types itself at your speed: "the lady Madeline (for so was she called) passed slowly through a remote portion of the apartment". Turn toward the men and the stranger looks up. The sentence finishes anyway: "without having noticed my presence, disappeared." The door closes. You are still there.

## 5. What the game can say that the tale can't

**The tale hears Madeline only as the echo of a book. The game makes the book the echo of her.**

The narrator reaches her only through texts: her physicians' diagnosis [u15], the textbook signs of "maladies of a strictly cataleptical character" [u30], the *Vigiliae Mortuorum* [u28], Canning's romance. When her coffin breaks, he calls the real sound "the echo… of the very cracking and ripping sound which Sir Launcelot had so particularly described" [u40]. For him the fiction is the original and the woman its echo, and a first-person tale can't get out of that order. A game can reverse it, because input comes before text: you act, and then a sentence arrives to explain you away. By the third, the player knows what the tale only implies: he hasn't missed her; he has been reading over her.

## 6. What only this tale could do

Usher has a reading aloud timed to a buried woman's escape: a clock and three named sounds. It has a one-axis map, from the vault beneath his bed [u29] up to the door, and a fissure that splits the house only after she arrives [u50]. And it has twins bound by "sympathies of a scarcely intelligible nature" [u30], which the epigraph makes an image: a hanging lute that sounds when touched. Poe changed Béranger's "*Mon* cœur" to "*Son* cœur", and *son* doesn't say whose heart it is. Roderick hears "her heart" [u48] because they are strung alike. Poe's other buried women are narrated by the men who mourn them; only in Usher does one climb back up into a reading.

## 7. The seed

**Keep the crack, give it to her, drop the fall.** The crack is Madeline's route along Poe's fissure [u06]; each act widens it until the moon comes through [u50]. "Read too fast and it falls" is the men's fear. In Usher, haste is the crime ("my haste" [u48]), and the house cannot fall before she reaches it. Pace survives as timing in the reading: to be heard at all, your acts must land on his sentences. Act early and the noise is lost "amid the rattling of the sashes" [u40], and the crack waits for the next sentence. No fail state; the only cost is going unheard.

## 8. Why it's small

The map has one axis and six spaces, most of them dark. About 2,000 words of Poe, chosen and laid out; Madeline needs no lines. Two inputs: hold to move or strain; release to be still. One ending, no branches, no saves. Eight beats of one to three minutes come to 15–20 minutes.

## 9. The risk

**Stillness that reads as waiting.** For two-thirds of the game Madeline can barely move. If her small acts don't visibly change the sentences, the player is watching Poe scroll past a black screen. Watch for testers who stop pressing, and any minute where input gets no written reply. The cure is the core rule: every act gets a sentence, even a wrong one. Being misread has to feel like being answered.

## 10. A sample moment (beat 5)

The coffin lid, a hand's breadth away. No light reaches the vault except a line of type, arriving overhead:

> "…uplifted his mace outright, and, with blows, made quickly room in the plankings of the door for his gauntleted hand;"

The player holds. The wood bows. The sentence goes on as the screws give, one word to each:

> "…he so cracked, and ripped, and tore all asunder…"

Split grain. Air. Silence above. Then, smaller, in the narrator's own hand:

> "…it appeared to me (although I at once concluded that my excited fancy had deceived me)… the echo (but a stifled and dull one certainly) of the very cracking and ripping sound which Sir Launcelot had so particularly described."

The line of light slides off across the stone. He has turned the page.
