# Perfect Keeping

## 1. Name and one-line pitch

**Perfect Keeping.** You are the House of Usher, stone and master under one name, and every sound made inside you is yours to keep in the walls or let through to Roderick's ear; whatever you keep, the house gives back. (Poe's "perfect keeping" of house and family [u04]; the vault was once a "donjon-keep" [u29].)

## 2. Whose experience

The player is the House of Usher in the peasants' "equivocal" sense [u04]: the mansion and the family as one body. Its faculty is the one Roderick claims for it: sentience sustained by "long undisturbed endurance" [u26]. Its nerve is Roderick, whose senses are so acute that "even the most trivial, incident" is an assault [u11–u12]. The epigraph states the rule: touch the lute and it resounds [u01].

This seat holds the tale's secret. The narrator learns last what the house knew first: "I heard her first feeble movements in the hollow coffin … many, many days ago" [u48]. He witnesses coincidences; the house is where they come from. And it cannot speak. Like the palace's Echoes [u23], it only passes sound on or holds it: "I dared not speak" given a body.

## 3. The arc

1. **Soundless day** [u02–u06]. A rider reins at the tarn and studies your reflection. A fissure runs from roof to water, barely perceptible. *Follows Poe.*
2. **The guest's steps** [u07–u13]. Trophies rattle; a stealthy valet; the physician on the stair. You learn the verb. Roderick's terms [u11] are the rules: strings soothe, everything else hurts, and each hurt makes the next one louder, by Poe's own law of terror [u05]. *Follows.*
3. **She passes** [u14–u15]. Madeline's slow step crosses the studio, and a door closes on her. He weeps. That night she takes to her bed. *Follows.*
4. **Painting and reading** [u16–u27]. The guest reads aloud and plays: sounds you *want* to carry. Roderick sings "The Haunted Palace", his gauge: six stanzas, radiant to red-litten. *Follows. The ballad becomes the interface.*
5. **The keep** [u28–u30]. Two men carry a coffin down through you. The iron door grates, and that grating is your own voice. *Follows.*
6. **Seven or eight days** [u31–u32]. Faint sounds rise from the deepest room, directly beneath the guest's bed. Roderick roams, listening. *Departs:* Poe gives these days one paragraph. The game plays them, and they are its longest stretch.
7. **The Mad Trist** [u33–u47]. The storm comes, and the glowing exhalation, your atmosphere made visible [u26, u35]. The guest reads Ethelred. You carry the reading, the best move left, and each of the romance's three noises draws out a sound you kept: the door, the shriek, the clang. *Departs:* Poe leaves room for coincidence. Here nothing is coincidence. It is return.
8. **The fall** [u48–u50]. Roderick's "many minutes, many hours, many days" describes your play. She stands at the door. The moon shines through the fissure you inked, and the tarn closes over the name. *Follows,* with one departure the system earns. Keep too much of her and the fissure reaches the water before the Trist. Then the house falls early, still holding her, and the last pages sink unread.

## 4. The first minute

The house stands in elevation at dusk above its inverted twin in the tarn. There is no audio, because every sound in this game is a written word. One line of Poe sits at the foot of the screen: *"During the whole of a dull, dark, and soundless day…"* A small rider comes to the brink, looks down, and the reflection shivers. He crosses.

In the hall, in small italics: *rattled.* It starts to climb the stairwell toward one high lit window, where a figure lies on a sofa. If you hold it, it sinks into the masonry and one zigzag of the fissure darkens. If you let it rise, the figure lifts a hand to his eyes and a word of the first stanza dims in the margin.

Next come *a stealthy step* and *with trepidation*. A door opens, and the account reads: "ushered me into the presence of his master."

## 5. What the game can say that the tale can't

**Keeping the peace and burying someone alive are one act at different sizes.**

The tale buries Madeline by one decision, with a "worldly reason" nobody disputes [u28]; the silence after arrives as a confession [u48], judged from outside. The game breaks that decision into dozens of small ones. Each is made for the kindest motive: sparing a man whom faint light tortures, by the rules he gave you. So the player becomes the one who dared not speak through care, not cruelty, and learns it from their own record. In the tale, the fissure is an omen. In the game, it is a ledger.

## 6. What only this tale could do

Poe fuses building and mind four times: the equivocal name [u04], the palace-as-head ballad [u20–u25], sentient collocation [u26], the fissure from roof to water [u06, u50]. Nothing needs inventing. The secret is acoustic: she was *heard*. The climax is already call-and-response, three read sentences answered by three noises [u39–u47]. The tale runs from "soundless" [u02] to "the voice of a thousand waters" [u50], so its whole arc is a silence filling up. Poe even supplies the feedback loop [u05], and he footnotes the house's sentience to real men of science [u51], which licenses playing the premise straight. In any other haunted house the building frightens people. Here it hears, and keeps quiet.

## 7. The seed

**Changed.** I keep the crack and the threat of not finishing, and drop "read too fast."

Speed is the wrong cause: Poe's fissure is there at first glance [u06] and widens when the family ends [u50], not when anyone hurries. A reading clock would also punish exactly the attentive reading Lukas's edition protects, which declines "timed reading" by name. Here the crack is made of what the house kept, so it measures silence, not pace. Keep too much and the house falls before you finish, and the remaining pages go under the tarn with her.

## 8. Why it's small

One screen: the house and its reflection, one drawing in two states. One verb: hold to keep; letting go is the default. About forty sound-words, all Poe's vocabulary. Eight beats paced by Poe's passages, with no clock. Two gauges and two endings, all from the tale and one rule. Characters act on Poe's rails: no dialogue tree, navigation or inventory. Fifteen to twenty minutes, one sitting.

## 9. The risk

**It turns into meter-balancing.** Two gauges and a stream of rising tokens invite optimizing; once players stop reading the words, her sounds become one more thing to tidy, and the ending plays as a gotcha.

**Watch** the first sound from the vault in playtest. If players don't pause, the stream is too fast or too numerical. Cut sounds until they do. Show no numbers, and never let the game pass comment on what the player did.

## 10. A sample moment

*The eighth night. The guest's candle is out. In the gallery, Roderick has stopped with his head inclined, "as if listening to some imaginary sound."*

Far below the guest's bed, behind copper and iron, a word you have not seen before:

*stirs*

It is smaller than the storm and written in another hand.

It begins to rise, through the coppered archway, through the floor beneath the sleepless guest, up the stair, toward him.

Hold it, and the fissure darkens one step nearer the water. Let it go, and the palace door loses a pearl.

The guest's account reads, calmly: *"I struggled to reason off the nervousness which had dominion over me."*

Eleven steps of the fissure are hers already.
