# Perfect Keeping (v2)

## What changed and why

The v1s read as four solemn complicity games; mine had the weakest verb, a keep-or-carry toll inviting meter-balancing. v2 keeps the seat, crack and acoustic secret but makes the verb a pleasure: the house *collects* its sounds and plays them back to score the guest's readings. Poe's coincidences become your performance, reviewed by the narrator as coincidence (act-then-sentence from 02; the Trist as cheap quest from 04). The claim moves from silence to art. You are the instrument, until it plays itself.

## 1. Name and pitch

**Perfect Keeping.** You are the House of Usher, an aesthete in stone. You catch the sounds made inside you and play them back to score the guest's readings aloud. Lately, the best material comes from the vault.

"Keeping": Poe's painterly word for harmony [u04], a collection, a keep [u29], keeping time.

## 2. Whose experience

The player is the house and its master under one "equivocal" name [u04]. The family turns everything into art: "works of exalted art" [u04], a painted idea [u17], a collapse set as a ballad [u19–u25]. The house shares the taste. Its sentience [u26] is footnoted, deadpan, to a bishop's *Chemical Essays* [u51]. The epigraph is the controls: touch and it resounds [u01]. The secret is acoustic: she was *heard* [u48]. The climax is already sound design [u39–u47].

## 3. The arc

1. **Soundless day** [u02–u06]. Silence, a rider at the tarn, an empty crack. *Poe.*
2. **Arrivals** [u07–u15]. The first catches: *rattled*, *a stealthy step*, *with trepidation*, Madeline's slow step, a door closing on her. Each catch is shelved in the crack. *Poe.*
3. **Rehearsals** [u16–u27]. The guest reads Roderick's library aloud and you score it: Gresset's convent parrot learning oaths, Holberg's hero falling through the earth. Apt sounds light the palace stanzas; crass ones make Roderick wince. *Departs:* in Poe they only "pored together".
4. **The keep** [u28–u30]. The burial. The iron door's "unusually sharp grating sound" [u29] is the best specimen you will ever catch. *Poe.*
5. **Seven or eight days** [u31–u32]. Sounds rise from below in another hand: *stirs*, *scratches*. Not the house's. Superb. Shelve them, or let Roderick hear. *Departs:* the days Poe summarises are played.
6. **The Mad Trist** [u36–u47]. Your big night. For the hermit's door and the dragon's shriek you play your best two, and the account calls it coincidence. Yours or hers? The game never says. For the shield you have nothing that rings, and it rings anyway. *Departs:* coincidence is your performance, until the instrument plays itself.
7. **The door** [u48–u49]. Roderick glosses your score back into her: "say, rather, the rending of her coffin." Footsteps you didn't make. The ebony jaws are your mouth [u23, u49]; you open them. "It was the work of the rushing gust." *Poe, with the gust exposed.*
8. **A thousand waters** [u50]. The fissure splits, and everything you kept sounds at once, "a long tumultuous shouting sound": each finale is the player's own collection. *Departs, gently.*

## 4. The first minute

Dusk: the house in ink, in cross-section, above its inverted twin. Silence, until you poke something and it answers in Poe's word: the draperies *rustled*, the trophies *rattled*. Catch the rising word and it lodges in the hairline crack, labelled like a museum card. A rider is *ushered* in, which the house enjoys. Upstairs, a figure on a sofa flinches at everything but strings.

## 5. What the game can say that the tale can't

**Set a real cry perfectly to a story and nobody hears it, least of all the artist.**

The noises fit the romance so well that the narrator files them as "the echo … of the very cracking and ripping sound which Sir Launcelot had so particularly described" [u40]. The fit is what makes them deniable. A reader watches that happen. A player *makes* the fit, enjoys it, is reviewed as coincidence, then hears Roderick read the score back as fact [u48]. Because the pleasure was real, the turn lands without a sermon: you made an excellent sound effect out of a woman.

## 6. What only this tale could do

A climax built as call-and-response [u39–u47]; one grating sound heard at burial, reading and confession [u29, u44, u48]; a reading list that is a repertoire [u27]; a family whose vice is exalted art [u04]; a soundtrack from "soundless" [u02] to "a thousand waters" [u50]; and the reading held directly above the vault [u29, u33].

Other gothic houses scare their guests. This one accompanies them.

## 7. The seed

**Changed, as in v1.** The crack becomes the shelf, and every kept sound inks one compartment, so collecting brings on the fall. Overfill it and the house falls before the tale ends, taking the unread pages with it. The cause is greed, not reading speed.

## 8. Why it's small

One screen, two verbs (catch, play), about twenty sound-words, four readings, Poe abridged plus under 400 words of labels, two endings from one rule: 12–18 minutes.

## 9. The risk

**The toy swallows the turn.** If scoring is fun enough, *stirs* becomes just another good sample. Her sounds must be visibly other: written in another hand, with the provenance "not the house." Watch a tester's first *stirs*: a laugh is fine, a pause better, indifference fatal.

## 10. A sample moment

*The Trist, third sound. On your shelf: rattled, a stealthy step, passionate tears, stirs.*

> "…fell down at his feet upon the silver floor, with a mighty great and terrible ringing sound."

*Rattled* is too small. *Passionate tears*, too wet. *Stirs* has been moving in its compartment all evening.

Nothing rings, so you play nothing.

A word you never caught rises from the deepest room, up the copper and through the floor beneath the guest's chair, written in another hand:

*clangorous*

The guest leaps to his feet. Roderick rocks on, undisturbed.

For the first time since the Ushers began, someone else is playing you.

## 11. The slice

**Build the Mad Trist:** about four minutes, after a thirty-second cold open at the tarn.

- **The shelf.** Six sounds, pre-filled, whose labels tell the first half of the tale: *rattled* (the hall, the guest's arrival), *grating* (the iron door, "the day we laid her there"), *stirs* (the deepest room, "not the house").
- **The performance.** The guest reads; you score. Two coincidences, the clang you didn't play, Roderick's gloss, the door, then your shelf sounding at once as the tarn closes.

One screen, fifteen passages, one twist: the tale in miniature, where play and horror are one act.
