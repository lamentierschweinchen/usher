# 04 · The reader, v2

## What changed and why

Side by side, the drafts preached one sermon: someone's silence keeps her buried. I cut mine (the bookmark) and kept what only the reader has: the book as a thing with rules. It's now a gamebook that refuses every choice in Poe's words, until the one book in the house that lets you play. The crack is your path through its sections. Borrowed: sounds as words rising from below (03); Roderick's reveal as book and echo reversed (02). The particulars toy went to 01.

## 1. Name and pitch

**Nevertheless.** Usher becomes an old gamebook that turns down every choice in Poe's own words, until the stormy night you read aloud the silliest book in the house, whose choices work, on a door under the floor.

## 2. Whose experience

The reader, holding a book with opinions. Usher's fatalism is a gamebook's railroad, and Roderick states its rule: "Thus, thus, and not otherwise" [u12]. The narrator's hedges are its refusals: "Nevertheless" [u03], "did not feel at liberty to dispute" [u28], "the coincidence alone" [u40]. Inside sits a real game, the "Mad Trist" (door, monster, prize, a rule on the wall [u42]), read aloud until it comes true [u40–u47]. Only the reader holds both books.

## 3. The arc

Numbered sections, italic choices, "turn to". Poe is never rewritten; choices and joins are ours. Your path is the fissure [u06].

1. **Nevertheless** [u02–u06]. The joke and the crack (see the first minute).
2. **The one who crosses** [u14–u15]. Madeline's sentence walks out through the margin. *To follow her* → "seen by me no more" [u15].
3. **The singular proceeding** [u28–u30]. *To dispute it* → "did not feel at liberty to dispute". *To look longer* → "rested not long upon the dead" [u30]. The joke stops being funny. The page turn screws down the lid, and her lines sink into the bottom margin.
4. **Seven or eight days** [u31–u32]. Words rise in the margin in another hand. One choice points to a section the book doesn't have. *Departs.*
5. **The Mad Trist** [u33–u47]. Lamp, casement, "one of your favorite romances" [u36]. A second book opens in blackletter, and this one plays: Canning accepts every brave choice. A word rising from below answers each victory. Lean in, and Roderick's murmur [u45] is just legible. *Departs: we write the rest of Canning in his "uncouth and unimaginative prolixity" [u37].*
6. **Say, rather** [u48–u49]. Roderick glosses your quest, triumph by triumph, as her escape. The last section offers no choice: "the work of the rushing gust" [u49].
7. **The tarn** [u50–u51]. The moon shines through your path; the tarn closes over the title. What floats is Poe's footnote [u51], the only star in a night with "no glimpse of the moon or stars" [u35].

## 4. The first minute

A cheap 1840s gamebook title page. §1: Poe's opening, cut short, then *To ride on to the house, turn to 3. To turn thy horse for home, turn to 2.* Everyone picks home. §2, entire: "Nevertheless, in this mansion of gloom I now proposed to myself a sojourn of some weeks" [u03]. *Turn to 3.* That's the laugh, and the rule. §3 offers to "scan the house more narrowly". Take it, and a hairline joins the numbers in the order you read them, a zigzag down the wall.

## 5. What the game can say that the tale can't

**The silliest book in the house is the only one that works, and enjoying it is what lets her out.**

A tale can only say nobody could have done otherwise. A gamebook can offer the choice and let Poe refuse it, until his good manners sound like a lid being screwed down. Then it hands you Canning's dreadful romance, where you get to be the hero. Poe quotes three bits of the Trist and sneers [u37]. The game lets you win the shield, then has Roderick tell you whose door you broke [u48]. Your pleasure isn't the evidence against you. It's the lever.

## 6. What only this tale could do

No other gothic tale contains its own bad game, complete with monster, loot and a win condition in rhyme: "Who slayeth the dragon, the shield he shall win" [u42]. It is read aloud as noise over the real event, then glossed set piece by set piece as a woman breaking out of a tomb [u48]. Around it runs a railroad whose refusals Poe already wrote.

## 7. The seed

**Keep the crack; drop the clock.** The crack is your route through the sections, "from the roof … down the wall" [u06]. Every reader's route differs, and every one reaches the tarn. Falling is finishing [u50].

## 8. Why it's small

About 40 short sections of Poe and 12 of Canning pastiche, all hypertext; one verb, turn to; sounds as words, so no audio; one ending; twelve to eighteen minutes.

## 9. The risk

**The refusal joke runs out before the dread arrives.** Cap Usher at six refusals, each a different Poe hedge, escalating from "Nevertheless" to the lid. Watch whether players still read the one at the vault. Second: a hypertext gamebook can look like "just a Twine"; the page-house, crack and rising words must make it a book nobody has seen.

## 10. A sample moment

*§12, blackletter: Poe's Trist [u39], cut at its hinges; choices ours.*

> And Ethelred, who was by nature of a doughty heart, and who was now mighty withal, on account of the powerfulness of the wine which he had drunken —

*To hold parley with the hermit, turn to 13. To uplift thy mace, turn to 14.*

You choose parley. You are a reasonable person.

> **13.** — waited no longer to hold parley with the hermit, who, in sooth, was of an obstinate and maliceful turn. *Turn to 14.*

> **14.** — he so cracked, and ripped, and tore all asunder —

One word rises out of the bottom margin, in a hand that isn't the printer's: *ripped*

Across the room, Roderick begins to bring his chair round toward the door.

## 11. The slice

Build the night: 60 seconds of §1–3 (joke and crack), then the Trist from the lamp at the door [u33] to the footnote [u51]. About 20 sections: three brave choices, three rising words, the murmur, Roderick's gloss, the door and the fall. Static HTML and CSS, no audio, no state beyond your path. Six to eight minutes: funny in twenty seconds, uncanny by minute five, finished. If time runs short, cut the murmur, never the gloss.
