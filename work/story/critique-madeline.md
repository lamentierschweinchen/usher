# Critique · Madeline seat

## 01 · The Cheerfulness of My Society (narrator)

**Strongest:** the explanations are kind, and they work. Roderick's eye relights and the guitar comes back, so reasonableness is a temptation, not a vice. The first minute, dragging Poe's sedges about [u02], is the most playful moment in the room.

**Weakest:** it is one choice, explain or attend, made a dozen times toward an ending already fixed ("Nothing saves Madeline"). Players will have the moral by the third choice, and the rest is a margin filling in. The draft names the morality-quiz risk and doesn't escape it.

**Misreading:** "blames her sounds on the draperies [u32]." In [u32] the furniture and draperies explain his *nervousness*; the "low and indefinite sounds" he never explains at all. He dresses and paces. My v1 made the same slip. Also, "Madeline is sealed in" leaves out that she is the one character who gets herself out [u48].

## 03 · Perfect Keeping (house)

**Strongest:** "every sound in this game is a written word," with no audio at all, and "The Haunted Palace" as a six-stanza gauge running from radiant to red-litten [u20–u25]. Both are original and cheap to build.

**Weakest:** the protagonist wants nothing. With no desire to pursue, the player tends two gauges, and the early-fall ending lands as the gotcha the draft itself fears. It has the most system in the room and the least mystery.

**Misreading:** the fail state, "the fissure reaches the water before the Trist," contradicts [u06]. At first sight the fissure already runs from the roof "until it became lost in the sullen waters of the tarn"; at the end it widens [u50]. Also, at [u16] the guest listens and Roderick plays; the guest doesn't "read aloud and play."

## 04 · I Continued the Story (reader)

**Strongest:** the page as the house: justified type for masonry, the fissure as a river of white, Poe's footnote asterisk as the only star [u35, u51]. It also has the best find in the room: the Trist is a decoy game with its rules written on a shield [u42]. It's the most natural fit for Lukas, and it will screenshot beautifully.

**Weakest:** the verb is turning a page, and the stake is guilt about stopping, so the only real choice is to stop playing. The e-book risk it names is real.

**Misreading:** "the potency of a spell" [u49] belongs to Roderick's shriek, not to the narrator's reading. More seriously, by making each sound wait "for your turn," it credits Madeline's escape to the reader. She gets out on her own [u48]; the reading only accompanies her.

## Bet

I'd back Madeline v2, because it's the only one of the four with an opponent and a punchline: you play against the narrator's word-for-word excuses, and Poe already wrote the line where they run out ("there *did* stand" [u49]). 04's page-as-house is the best form in the room, and the winning build probably sets that game on that page.
