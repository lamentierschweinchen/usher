# Merely Electrical Phenomena

*Story direction 01, v2 · the narrator's seat*

## What changed and why

Side by side, the four drafts were solemn games about complicity. Mine was the most solemn: explain or attend, with an obvious right answer. But the narrator is the one funny person in Usher ("merely electrical phenomena" [u36]; "Such opinions need no comment" [u26]), so explaining is now the game itself, a craft played with Poe's own excuses. The mystery survives because he, unlike Madeline, doesn't know. Credited: phenomena arriving as written words (03); every act getting a sentence (02). Fixed: Poe's rearrangement happens in the tarn.

## 1. Name and one-line pitch

**Merely Electrical Phenomena.** A detective game in reverse: as Roderick's sensible friend, you account for every uncanny thing in the house with one of Poe's own explanations, until they run out and she is at the door.

## 2. Whose experience

The narrator is Poe's anti-detective. Two years before Dupin (1841), Poe wrote a man who holds every clue and connects none: a "suspiciously lingering smile" [u30], a vault beneath his own bed [u29], sounds "at long intervals" [u32]. Summoned to cheer Roderick [u03], he explains, calmly and usually truly. The player plays that talent. The pleasure is being good at it; the dread is the back page.

## 3. The arc

**The rule.** Uncanny things arrive as words in italics (from 03): *rattled*, *a vapour*. The narrator carries a case of explanations, each a phrase of Poe's: *the draperies*, *the storm*, *the miasma of the tarn*, *catalepsy*, *coincidence*. Set one that fits beside a phenomenon and Poe's sentence writes itself, and Roderick settles. Explanations wear out. Whatever you can't or won't explain goes onto the back page, under *Unaccounted for*: the solution, assembling itself.

1. **The tarn [u02–u06].** Causeless gloom: "considerations beyond our depth" [u02]. For the fissure, the case offers *a scrutinizing observer* [u06]: blame someone who isn't there.
2. **The studio [u07–u15].** Roderick explains himself: "a mere nervous affection" [u11]. Madeline crosses [u14]; nothing fits *dread*, so it goes on the back page.
3. **The days [u16–u27].** Easy explaining, plus one wild card: *No comment* [u26].
4. **The vault [u28–u30].** *A fortnight* is *a harmless precaution* [u28]. *A faint blush* is *catalepsy*, a perfect fit [u30]. The right explanation is the horror.
5. **The nights [u31–u32].** A departure: four nights instead of Poe's one. Sounds beneath the bed; the draperies wear thin.
6. **The reading [u33–u47].** The glow outside is *electrical phenomena* [u36]. The Mad Trist's sounds are *coincidence*, then a "most extraordinary" *coincidence* [u45], and then nothing fits [u47].
7. **The door [u48–u49].** Roderick delivers the detective's summing-up and calls the reasonable man "Madman!" The last explanation fits: *the rushing gust* [u49]. It's true. She's there anyway.
8. **The causeway [u50].** The fissure returns, moonlit. The case is empty, and the tarn closes over the name. The last screen is the back page: the confession he never wrote.

Events are Poe's; departures: the case, the four nights, the back page.

## 4. The first minute

A letter in Roderick's agitated hand. The case opens: eight explanations, laid out like surgical instruments. The house at dusk, with *insufferable gloom* written over the roof. Try *the draperies* and the card slides back with a dry note: there are no draperies on a façade. Look into the tarn, as Poe's narrator does: the phrase doubles, upside down. *Considerations beyond our depth* fits: Poe's sentence appears, stamped ACCOUNTED FOR. Then, in the reflection: *a fissure*. Explain it with *a scrutinizing observer*, or leave it: the back page's first line.

## 5. What the game can say that the tale can't

**You can be right about every detail and wrong about the woman.**

Poe's narrator never lies. The gust did open the doors [u49]; storms do rattle sashes [u40]; catalepsy does leave "the mockery of a faint blush" [u30]. In print, that reads as sanity. A game can hand the player his explanations, let them get good at it, and still bring her to the door. The truth gets written only on the back page, by what you failed to explain.

## 6. What only this tale could do

Usher is the anti-detective story Poe wrote two years before he invented the detective, and in it the madman delivers the summing-up [u48]. At the climax the narrator uses a cheap romance [u37] to explain away three real sounds, hearing the real noise as "the echo" of the book's [u40] (02's point). And his last excuse is true while she stands in the doorway [u49]: the joke and the horror in one sentence.

## 7. The seed

**Keep the crack as the first and last phenomenon; drop the clock.** The fissure is the first thing he explains away [u06] and the last thing he sees [u50]. His fault is composure, not haste, and Lukas's edition rules out timed reading. The pressure is the case thinning.

## 8. Why it's small

One verb: set an explanation beside a thing, or don't. About twenty-five phenomena and eight to ten explanations, all in Poe's phrases. One drawing per beat. Original writing: the letter, the dry notes, the labels. No fail state: the case runs dry at the Mad Trist by design. Fifteen to twenty minutes.

## 9. The risk

**Keyword matching.** Players drop explanations by label without reading, and the wit dies. Watch whether they read the sentence each fit produces; if not, make fit depend on the whole sentence. The dry notes carry the comedy; they must never wink.

## 10. A sample moment

*The door [u48–u49].* "Madman! I tell you that she now stands without the door!"

The huge antique panels throw slowly back their ponderous and ebony jaws. A word fills the doorway: ***open***

One explanation is left: *the rushing gust.*

You set it beside the doors. It fits.

Your account writes: "It was the work of the rushing gust —"

ACCOUNTED FOR.

Then the sentence goes on without you: "— but then without those doors there *did* stand the lofty and enshrouded figure of the lady Madeline of Usher."

## 11. The slice

**The seventh or eighth night [u32–u49], about seven minutes.** A back page already filled in (*low cunning*, *a fortnight*, *a faint blush*, *a lingering smile*) sets up the mystery at a glance. The player explains away the night's sounds and the glow at the window, then reads the Mad Trist as the case runs dry, ending on the true last explanation. One room, ten phenomena, seven explanations. It holds the whole idea in one night.
