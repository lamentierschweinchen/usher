# Shadows

*Game designer and shadow-theatre lead, 31 October 2026. Poe cited by block id.*

**In short.** One recurring mechanism, *A Mere Different Arrangement*: you move cut-outs on rods before a lamp, and their shadows are what's there. The tarn returns every shadow as light, and below, light is floor. The sleepwalker replaces the door's slide puzzle; its far end, a rod held from below, becomes act two's interlude verb. The best new scene is *The Haunted Palace*, the ballad as a shadow play, with her collapse falling across your screen.

## What I played

I played the door, flight, tarn and burial, and later the studio (both ways), nights and trist (revised; verdicts at the end).
- **Door: replace the puzzle.** I dragged the gap three stones and the red route drew itself in the water. I was tracing a line, not choosing one.
- **Flight: replace the game.** It walks for you. I held one key, read *Alone behind me.*, and a counter ticked: the best light in the game, and nothing to do in it.
- **Keep the rest.** The tarn's cheat and the burial's lure are real; nights is the high point.

## 1. A recurring mechanism

To build on: a shadow looks like a thing, not like what cast it; nearer the lamp, it grows and softens; and this look's tarn already returns every black cut as light.

### A. The Lamp
*You hold the light; what it throws on the wall is what the guest writes down.*
- **First minute.** On the stair the valet hands you his candle. Held low, the physician's shadow climbs the wall, huge, all "low cunning and perplexity" [u07]; held high, he's a small worried man with a bag.
- **Chapters.** The stair; the studio, where light tortures Roderick [u11]; the nights, where he "carefully shaded his lamp" [u34]; the flight's moon.
- **Only this tale.** The physician's tell (lit from below, a villain) becomes your doing.
- **Risk.** A dial, not a puzzle; it gives that tell away; every horror game has a lamp.

### B. A Mere Different Arrangement
*You move cut-outs on rods before a lamp; their shadows are what's there.*
- **First minute.** The first interlude: a lamp, and on the wall a woman's shadow where no one stands. A coat on a stand, a chair and a drape hang on rods from the foot of the stage. Drag the coat before the lamp and its shadow swallows hers; the Account writes the coat. To cover her it stood nearer the lamp, so it's too big and soft at the edge. Or leave her be.
- **The grammar.** Two gestures: drag a rod to place a thing; tap to bring it nearer the lamp. Whatever moves without a rod is the house, or her.
- **Chapters, transformed.** Interlude one: cut-outs to hide her. II: to build her a way (§2). The Haunted Palace (§3): the lamp moves by itself, and fair cut-outs become monsters. IV: the physician's lamp throws the bearers on the copper wall, and the coffin has three. VII: the setting moon is the lamp, the house's shadow the pursuer. **Act two:** below, light is floor, so what you cast above, she stands on, and what you hung to explain her away, she knocks at. The bible's "your excuses are her walls" gets a shape.
- **Only this tale.** It's the narrator's own theory, that "a mere different arrangement of the particulars of the scene" might "annihilate its capacity for sorrowful impression" [u02], tested at once on the tarn's "re-modelled and inverted images". Roderick adds that the house lives by "the order of their arrangement" and "its reduplication in the still waters of the tarn" [u26]. Whatever you arrange, the water doubles and the house keeps.
- **Risk.** Fiddly on phones; puzzle tedium; *Shadowmatic* did shadow-shaping first. Hence two gestures and small rooms.

### C. The Rods
*Every figure is a puppet, and the water shows whose hand.*
- **First minute.** In the tarn the figures come back with their rods: the valet's runs into the wall, the guest's toward you, Madeline's down into the water.
- **Chapters.** All: follow a rod to see who moves whom; in act two, take hers.
- **Only this tale.** Catalepsy as puppetry: the body above in a trance, worked from below.
- **Risk.** It answers what the bible keeps open (is the valet the house?) and makes every chapter puppetry.

**I'd pick B,** because only there is a shadow something you build with, so it makes puzzles rather than readings; because the theory is Poe's, not ours; and because it crosses the waterline by a rule the look already keeps. It takes the best of the others: A's lamp becomes its second dial, and C's rods its handles and one act-two reveal, kept to Madeline so the valet stays open.

**For ELEMENTS, to take or leave** (revised). An excuse can be a cut-out whose shadow must reach what it explains (ELEMENTS' *reach*, as physics: nearer the lamp it reaches further, and blurs), with a life below (a wall she knocks down). The Account writes the wall, not the room. *Shade* means silhouette, ghost and lampshade.

## 2. The sleepwalker

**The verb: set the room.** You move cut-outs on rods; she walks their shadows by herself. Her rules: she walks on shadow, never on light; she keeps walking and turns at a wall; she climbs any slope a stair could be; at a drop she stops, arms out. Catalepsy makes it exact: her body walks above in a trance while she is below, which is why she passes "without having noticed my presence" [u14].

**First minute.** Chapter II, the guest's room, the evening she "succumbed" [u15]. The valet sets down the lamp: "The family never venture forth, sir" (VOICE's line, after [u13]; revised). A gauze figure comes through the wall, arms out, walking the wainscot's shadow toward a gap of lamplight. The chair's rod catches the light. Drag it, and its back's shadow bridges the gap; she walks over it, and on.

**Escalation** (five minutes, one idea per wall):
1. **Bridge.** One gap, one chair.
2. **Reach.** The lamp has a rod too. Lower it and every shadow climbs: the chair-back becomes a ladder to a window "altogether inaccessible from within" [u08]. But mending one gap opens another.
3. **The house edits** (revised). While you hold one rod, something moves without one: that's the house, steering her the long way.
4. **The floor mirrors.** The ebon floor [u07] returns the wall as light, upside down and a moment early: you see her fall before she falls, and that the window above is, in the floor, a way down.
5. **The choice.** Up to the window, every piece where the house wants it (the long way), or down the bed's shadow to the vault "immediately beneath" your room [u29] (her short way, the room askew).

**No retries.** At a drop she waits; after 15 seconds the house moves a piece, and the Account counts it.

**Both ways.** Below, the reveal is a rod running from the gauze body down through the floorboards into the glass, where Madeline holds it. Now you walk the body yourself while your recorded hand re-sets the room on its old timings: bridges arrive when you placed them; the coat stands where you hung it. Her verb is the knock: rap the rod on a cut-out that hid her, and if no excuse can explain a knock, it falls. Above, you set the stage for a walker you couldn't steer; below, you steer her on a stage you can't set.

**Where: chapter II, replacing the slide puzzle.** It keeps the title, the liar (the tarn mirrors, now in the floor), the choice, the flags and the reveal: the door's own idea, Madeline as the gap you walk, given a verb beyond tracing. Its lower half becomes act two's interlude verb: a minute walking her body through a room you set above.

**Risk.** A borrowed genre: *Lemmings*, Ocean's *Sleepwalker*, *Projection: First Light*. Ours differs where it counts: you never steer her above, the hint is the tarn's, and below you hold the rod against yourself. Against waiting on a slow walker: one idea per wall, under 40 seconds of walking.

## 3. New scenes

Of fourteen, I killed ten: gags (hand shadows), duplicates (stealth on the stair), settled ground (the hinge as magic lantern), gotchas (twin lamps), mere images, and anything touching nights.

### The Haunted Palace
*Roderick sings his ballad and you stage it in his toy theatre, while the lamp creeps closer.*
- **First minute.** A paper theatre on the studio table, a lamp behind it, shades on rods: palace, banners, windows, spirits, throne, door. Roderick plucks, and "In the greenest of our valleys" [u20] lights. Raise the palace; it stands fair.
- **Escalation** (revised). You stage only the stanzas you've earned (ELEMENTS: comfort buys I–IV, candour V–VI), placing what he sings. For comfort, Roderick shades the lamp himself [u34] and the palace stays fair. For candour, the lamp comes in a notch a stanza, the palace's shadow becomes a head in profile, and your spirits turn "vast forms that move fantastically" [u25]. Then a shadow you aren't working crosses the screen and falls: her collapse, inside your show, and you took it for a puppet. Play on (**Nevertheless.**) and the lamp comes a stanza nearer, or go round the screen to look.
- **Where.** After III, replacing the one-minute collapse with about three. Below, a minute behind the screen, mirrored: her hand on the rod of the body that falls.
- **Only this tale.** The ballad is already a shadow play through windows, "Spirits moving musically" [u22] turned to vast forms. Its turn is a lamp's distance.
- **Risk.** A karaoke chore: so four placements, one line a stanza, and the lamp does the drama. The "red-litten windows" stay ember (red is never light).

### Its Shadows Alone
*The flight, rebuilt round its one light: "the vast house and its shadows were alone behind me" [u50].*
- **First minute.** The setting moon shines through the fissure, laying a red road across the causeway that zigzags as the crack does [u06]. You run; drag to stay on the road. Whatever lies in the house's shadow is the house: the hushed taps, your excuses, then her, reaching from the road's edges. Hold to look back, as now, and they freeze, but the moon sinks a notch and every shadow lengthens.
- **Escalation.** Three runs, as in the bible. The walls part and the road splits; the water's road runs early to show which half holds. Then the moon's whole orb bursts through [u50], and her shadow comes down the road.
- **Below.** The house's shadow returns as light, so she runs on what chases you, moving only while your recorded self looks back.
- **Only this tale.** One light, setting and blood-red, through a zigzag crack.
- **Risk.** A runner, and short again: three runs of about 75 seconds, one new idea each.

### Held to the Lamp
*The library, played: read by the light through the page.*
- **First minute.** The *Vigiliae Mortuorum* [u27] lies open at a harmless rite. Hold the page to the lamp: its back shows through, reversed, and between the lines the custom forms. Below, each earlier guest's Account bears the house as a watermark, upside down.
- **Only this tale.** A guest who writes, a house that reverses.
- **Risk.** Clever wordplay is hard; ELEMENTS owns it, and this is only a surface.

### The Pinhole
*Inside the coffin, every screw hole is a camera.*
- **First minute.** Black, and four pricks of light in the lid, each throwing the vault upside down onto its inside: torches, Roderick's hands at his ears, the physician in the doorway, your hat bent over a screw. The beams cross on what she wrote. With each screw you turned, one goes out; backing the screws out reopens them on an empty vault.
- **Where.** IV below, a minute before the screws.
- **Only this tale.** The vault is "entirely without means of admission for light" [u29]; Roderick's tunnel, lit "in a ghastly and inappropriate splendor" with no torch [u18], is a pinhole seen from inside.
- **Risk.** A viewing scene; keep it to a minute.

**I'd build Its Shadows Alone first:** it mends the weakest game, at the climax. **The Haunted Palace is the best new scene:** the ballad, her collapse and comfort-or-candour in one played moment. The Pinhole is cheap. Held to the Lamp waits on ELEMENTS.

## After reading the others

**1. What I'd adopt.**
- *VOICE:* Madeline cuts the guest's line once and keeps the tail: a cut, the look's own act, made from below. Its hinge, *Immediately beneath.*, names the sleepwalker's room: yours, over the vault. Its door line is now in §2.
- *ELEMENTS:* reach. "The draperies don't reach below the floor" is a shadow's property: an excuse explains only what its shadow covers. Excuse cards become cut-outs with no new rule; its "ghost as terrain" is my sleepwalker below.

**2. What I'd kill.**
- *VOICE:* "never 'tap'". Teaching lines in the tale's verbs (*harken*, *let be*) name no gesture; a first-timer on a phone needs the action named once. Poe for labels; the mime hand teaches.
- *ELEMENTS:* Echo's cut at a syllable: fine-motor work on a phone, and three of six cuts are the same *un-*, a quiz. Snap cuts to VOICE's joints.
- *Mine, conceded:* *no lace* as a tell (too fine; now "moves without a rod") and HE DIED, which ELEMENTS had killed. Both revised.

**3. Conflicts.**
- *Act two's map: one inside the other.* The shadow stage is the map; the Account is what stands on it. Each front-page line hangs in the reflected house as the cut-out it named, a wall; each back-page item is a gap of light, a door. The sheet shows only when someone writes: her echo strikes a line, its cut-out drops, and the page appears, struck.
- *Comfort and candour: yes.* The Haunted Palace stages only the stanzas earned (revised): comfort's palace stays fair; candour's lamp comes in, and her fall reads as one of the vast forms. The scene's choice is **Nevertheless.**: play on, and the lamp comes a stanza nearer, or look behind the screen.

**4. Honesty check.** The pane was hidden, so I drove the studio on its manual clock.
- *Studio: held, and better.* An idle act one now loses. Act two is the best thing built: my recorded hand caught both my taps, and the game said why (*Wait till it's busy.*). It's the sleepwalker's model below.
- *Nights: held.* A grating beneath the floor rewrote itself to fit my card: *Quite so.*
- *Trist: held, with one fix.* It took seven blows to break a plank: the aim runs from the mace, and nothing says so. I reached the dragon, not the page turn.
