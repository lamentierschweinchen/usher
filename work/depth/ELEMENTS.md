# Depth in every element

*Elements specialist, 31 October 2026. Poe cited by block id.*

## 1. The depth test

An element stays only if it passes all four.

1. **It changes the next move:** the player decides something that costs (comfort, a screw, a line, a second).
2. **It returns three times:** taught, turned on the player in act one, handed to Madeline in act two. Shown once, it's a gotcha; never changed, a chore.
3. **Two readers, one act:** the first-timer gets a decision without Poe; the Poe reader recognises Poe's sentence *in that decision*, not in a caption beside it.
4. **Only it:** name the one thing it does that nothing else does. Two elements with the same name merge.

A **gotcha** is a reveal with no next move; a **chore**, a move with no reveal; **noise**, neither. Depth is a reveal that changes the next move.

## 2. The elements

### Red, "not so": deepen

- **Do:** red is a voice the guest keeps putting out. Act one pays you to silence it: hushing the red *tap.* scores best; the fourth screw shuts the red sliver. Act two hands you the red: keep it alive against your recorded hand, and red that survives strikes a line of your Account.
- **Readers:** what you were paid to put out was a person; for the Poe reader, her red is "say, rather" [u48], Roderick's grammar for the truth.
- **Fork:** (A) red in three hands, as now; (B) red is hers alone; the water disagrees in mirror-writing, Roderick by echo (§3). **I'd pick B:** nobody tells three slants apart on a phone, so A collapses into "red is the answer", the oracle Lukas rejected. She stays unreliable: below, she's the looker the water flatters.
- **Fails as:** a gotcha, if red is always right.

### The Account: deepen

- **Do:** it appears only when someone writes in it (you, Roderick at supper, the hinge, her), and the scores are its lines.
- **Arc (revised):** below, it's the key to her map, which is SHADOWS' stage: every line names a cut-out standing there, the front page's as walls, the back page's (*Unaccountable* [u32]) as open doors. She knocks, your ghost answers with the line, and if her echo (§3) lands, the line is struck and the cut-out falls. Struck lines plus back-page items open the endings.
- **Readers:** "my diary lies, and I wrote it." Open it on Poe's first sentence [u02], and the shelf of earlier Accounts becomes a shelf of "The Fall of the House of Usher".
- **Fails as:** a chore, read rather than written.

### Excuse cards and the guest's *m.*: deepen the cards; merge *m.* into them

- **Do:** every card has a reach from the first interlude, as in the nights ("The draperies don't reach below the floor"): explaining is fitting, and a misfit bounces. The house keeps what you play: next morning the drape hangs there. Swap *family custom* (not Poe's) for *a harmless precaution* [u28]; *catalepsy* is the one true card, used to stop looking.
- ***m.*** (for *merely* [u36]): dither and the guest plays the likeliest card, and where it doesn't fit, the evidence bends to it, seeding chapter V's trick.
- **Arc:** below, each excuse is in her way, and her forgeries wear your cards to *most extraordinary coincidence* [u45], then blank. Your cards answer her knock on time; his *m.* cards late, since nobody was listening.
- **Readers:** a puzzle of reach; the narrator's own excuses [u32, u36, u40]. **(revised)** They're SHADOWS' cut-outs: an excuse big enough to cover her stands too near the lamp, too large and never sharp, the guest's tell made physical.
- **Fails as:** a chore, if it stays a menu.

### Blanks filled by dragging: merge into the Account; cut to three

The 6:30 blank asks for names the first-timer hasn't met, and teaches waiting. Keep three, filled with a figure you saw, never a word: the portrait's face into *___ went down*, the bare feet into *Bearers: ___*, and below, her into *P.S. in the hand of ___*.

### **Nevertheless.**: simplify to one job

- **Do:** it labels the guest's way on at each fork, and nowhere else: *Turn back*, "Before anyone does anything", the fourth screw, closing the casement [u36]. The other way is to let it be. The Account counts them.
- **Arc:** at the hinge it turns hers, the same word meaning the opposite: his "I'll stay and see nothing" [u03], her "in spite of the lid". Below, it's how she gets up after a hush.
- **Readers:** a word the first-timer catches themselves saying; the narrator's first decision [u03], made at every fork.
- **Fails as:** noise, once it labels an ordinary *next*.

### The tarn's rules: keep three, one per scene

- **Do:** *early* is a forecast you act on (three bearers in the water before IV); *mirror*, a text you read (the P.S., the water's remarks, §3); *flattery*, what to discount, checked from a window where you can't be in the picture.
- **Arc:** taught one per scene in minutes 2–12; used against you (the door's swapped sides; you gallant mid-coffin, beside the third bearer); turned below, where she's the looker.
- **Readers:** a forecast and a trap; "the re-modelled and inverted images" [u02].
- **Fails as:** arbitrary, the day a fourth rule appears.

### The recording and the ghost: keep; vary its job

- **Do:** so it never becomes "watch your replay", give the ghost a new job each turn: opponent (studio), terrain (door), witness (burial: your glance at the lid), ally (flight: she moves only while you look back), voice (she echoes your Account).
- **Readers:** "that's me" needs no Poe; the Poe reader sees your ghost pass her "without having noticed my presence" [u14].
- **Fails as:** a rerun, or a weak opponent after an idle act one.

### Comfort and candour: merge into the Account; the guitar is its face

Comfort counts excuses (the front page), candour things left be (the back): a second ledger. The guitar shows what Roderick read; no number on screen.
- **Do, with Poe (revised):** the poem is played once, in SHADOWS' *Haunted Palace*, and the lamp starts where your pages put it: comfort holds it to the bright stanzas (I–IV [u20–23]), candour lets it reach the dark (V–VI [u24–25]); the scene's own choice moves it one stanza. A comfort player never hears "evil things, in robes of sorrow", which the water reads as *live things* (§3).
- **Arc:** comfort eases act one and hardens act two; his omission tell becomes the echo she inherits.
- **Readers:** a friend who sings or stops; Poe's "speaking guitar" [u16], and which half of the poem you earned.
- **Fails as:** a meter to optimise.

### One liar per chapter: keep the frame; cut tells that don't return

- **Keep** II the tarn mirrors, IV the physician lit from below, V the evidence agrees, VII you; and I, the house edits, which was her. **(revised)** Cut *no lace* for SHADOWS' rule: whatever moves without a rod is the house; her edits keep the gauze rim.
- **III, Roderick omits:** hands to his ears, and the echo. **VI, the book echoes:** the echo running early [u40].
- **The valet's copperplate:** cut as a hand; it's the house keeping your excuses. *Is he the house?* stays open.
- **Readers:** a rule a chapter; each liar is the narrator's line, like the physician's "low cunning" [u07].
- **Fails as:** seven gotchas in a row ("so the physician was nice").

### Five ending slots: simplify

Keep five, each hinged on one act the Account names: the fourth screw, her KNOCK heard, candour, the gust, the look back. No hidden counts: the secret becomes the fourth screw left out and, on the causeway, a look down, not back. Each empty slot names the choice you didn't make ("Someone dared speak."); the last card ends on the echo (§3).
- **Readers:** what else could have happened; Poe's fall is just slot four, the default.
- **Fails as:** replay bait; as confession it holds.

## 3. The wordplay mechanics

Poe was already clever with words; the work is finding where his sentences say two things, and giving the player the act that makes the second heard. Of seven candidates I killed four: **what the water carries** (only capitals symmetric top to bottom survive: SHE comes back HE, one shiver, then a lipogram); **the snip** (free cuts say anything); **who's "he"?** (the blank again); **say, rather, matched** (a quiz).

### A. Echo

*She can only give back the end of what you said, and the end is where the guest's denials come undone.*

**The first minute** (IV, below). In the dark coffin, your Account comes down through the lid, a cut-paper line in your hand: *the yet unscrewed lid.* One teaching line: *She can't speak first. Answer him.* Run a finger along the strip; a red nick follows. Let go after *un*, and the tail rises in her red: *screwed lid.* Above, the line is struck; four screw tips show through the wood, to back out one per heartbeat. Nick it elsewhere (*lid*) and the echo is noise your ghost writes off: *the house settling.*

| Poe's line | Cut | Echo | What it does, and how it lands |
|---|---|---|---|
| "the yet unscrewed lid" [u30] | un· | *screwed lid.* | Strikes *Screws turned*. The word confessed by itself. |
| "You must not — you shall not behold this!" [u36] | — | Roderick: "…behold this!" | Act one teaches it: he echoes the guest, and looks. He agreed, and meant the opposite. |
| "her name was unmentioned by either Usher or myself" [u16] | un· | *mentioned.* | Nights below: her name enters the Account. You never once wrote it. |
| *Unaccountable* [u32], the back page's head (revised, after VOICE) | Un· | *accountable.* | Every back-page item is signed in her hand. The doors were her, and you answer for them. |
| "I *dared* not speak!" [u48], Roderick recorded | not· | *speak.* | If he had a true line from you, he answers: the Swap opens. She asks in his words. |
| "the fragments of the 'House of Usher'" [u50] | Us· | *her.* | The last card: the tarn closes and gives back one word. |

- **Only this tale:** Roderick's confession opens with an echo that strips a denial: "Not hear it? — yes, I hear it" [u48]. The palace has "a troop of Echoes" [u23]; Echo pined for Narcissus, the looker who loved his reflection. And the narrator denies by prefix, in some thirty *un-* words, so his sentences carry their contradiction in their tails.
- **Arc:** Roderick teaches it; the Trist runs it early; she uses it; the tarn ends on it.
- **The risk:** supply (a dozen great tails: one per turn and the ending, not every line); triviality, if it's always "after *un*" (vary the cut; give each line two true but useless tails); reading (eight words a line at most).

### B. Turn the word

*Poe's double meanings as two-sided cut-outs: turn one over and the sentence says the other thing.*

**The first minute** (the gallery). *The family lay in the direct line of descent* [u04]. One word catches the light. Turn *descent*, and its shadow changes from a family tree to a stair going down.

| Poe's line | Word | Front / back |
|---|---|---|
| "both the family and the family mansion" [u04] | House | stones / twins |
| "the direct line of descent" [u04] | descent | lineage / going down |
| "she now stands without the door" [u48] | without | outside / with no door |
| "looked upon the face of the tenant" [u30] | tenant | occupant / lodger on a fortnight's lease |
| "we two alone bore it to its rest" [u29] | rest | repose / what's left |

- **Only this tale:** Poe names the device, "the quaint and equivocal appellation" [u04], and ends on the name in quotation marks [u50].
- **Lands as:** a nod from the Poe reader; a footnote with a picture for the first-timer, since most second senses are archaic. **The risk:** the verb is thin (find the lit word, turn it).
- **Verdict:** the richest vein, the weakest verb. Kill it; harvest the finds for the voice and as echo targets.

### C. The tarn reads backwards

*In the water, words read right to left; a few come back as other words, one unchanged.*

**The first minute.** The first look down: the tale's first paragraph floats reversed, gibberish but for one word. *Reined* comes back *denier*.

| Poe's line | Above | In the water |
|---|---|---|
| "I reined my horse to the precipitous brink of a black and lurid tarn" [u02] | reined | *denier*: at his first look, the water names him |
| "a constitutional and a family evil" [u11] | evil | *live* |
| "But evil things, in robes of sorrow" [u24] | evil things | *live things*, in her white robes [u49] |
| "from its image in the pool" [u05] | pool | *loop*: the same evening, twice |
| "there *did* stand the lofty and enshrouded figure" [u49] | *did* | *did*: in capitals, the tale's one word that survives both the water's flips, backwards and upside down; Poe italicised it |

- **Only this tale:** it opens on a man studying a house upside down in water [u02].
- **Lands as:** a laugh at *denier*, a shiver at *did*. **The risk:** a word search, a chore to hunt, and most reversed text is noise.
- **Verdict:** merely cute as a system. Kill it; plant three as the water's remarks: *denier* at the first look, *live things* at stanza V, *did* at the doors in VI.

**I'd pick Echo** because a first-timer can say its rule in one breath, it turns the player's own sentences against him, it's Poe's move before ours, and it runs the whole arc. The other two survive as its content. In a shadow mechanism, the Account's lines are cut-paper strips lowered through the boards, and the echo is the cut.

## 4. What to cut

The gotchas and chores, each argued above: the unused Nevertheless page; red in three hands; *no lace* and the copperplate as tells; the 6:30 blank, and blanks as a standing verb; the Account after every chapter; *family custom*; counted secrets behind the endings; and garnish that needs a footnote (the playtime word underlined in [u48], the white river down the last Account). The asterisk star stays: it's a picture.

## After reading the others

**Adopt.** From VOICE: the person mirror, with Madeline's lid line *You have put me living in his tomb* [u48]; and *Unaccountable* [u32] for the back page, whose echo is *accountable* (revised in §3). From SHADOWS: *A Mere Different Arrangement*, the narrator's own theory [u02] made a verb, and its free tell: whatever moves without a rod is the house (replaces *no lace*; revised).

**Weakest.** VOICE: *Differences: ~~five~~ none.* It swaps the first-timer's favourite line ("Five of five. The house thanks you.") for a correction, a reveal with no next move. Keep hers. SHADOWS: *The Pinhole*, a viewing scene with no decision, fails test 1. Fold it into the screws: each one she backs out opens a pinhole on the vault, so the picture is earned.

**Act two's map: one inside the other.** The shadow stage is the map; the Account is its key. Every line names a cut-out standing there. She knocks on it (SHADOWS), your ghost answers with the line, she echoes it, and if the echo lands the line is struck and the cut-out falls (the Account, revised).

**Madeline's hand: one verb.** She plays the echo. VOICE's strike-one-write-one stays, but as each turn's opening reveal, written for her: let the player choose the written word and it's the blank again. The echo on three lines:
- "*We have put her living in the tomb!*" [u48], Roderick's cry → *living in the tomb.*
- "the yet unscrewed lid" [u30] → *screwed lid.*
- *Unaccountable* [u32], your back page → *accountable.*

**Comfort and candour: they fit,** if the poem is played once, in SHADOWS' scene, with the lamp starting where your pages left it and the scene's choice moving it a stanza. Drop the bible's stanza a chapter (comfort, revised).

**The test on SHADOWS.**
- *A Mere Different Arrangement:* **pass.** You build, it returns three ways (hide her, build her a way, her floor below), and only it lets you make the thing you then explain away.
- *The sleepwalker:* **pass, narrowly.** Its "only it" is real (you set a stage for someone you can't steer, which is the guest's whole position), but it lives or dies on walks under 40 seconds, and its step 3 must use the rod tell, not lace.
