The Depth Round

Three specialists worked on Poe's voice, depth in every element, and the shadows. They wrote separately, then read and critiqued each other's work. They agreed on more than I expected, and where they fought, the fight improved the idea. Below: each thread's options with my pick, what changes in the journey, my own read, and six calls.

Thread one

Poe's voice

The tension: Poe's sentences are long and the screen holds a line or two.

Option A

Recital

Every line is Poe's own, recut, re-addressed or misread.

RiskPoe gave the servant, the physician, Madeline and the custom no words, so the narrator ends up narrating everyone: a wall of text in instalments.

Option B

Ventriloquy

New lines in his diction and rhythm.

RiskPastiche. Nothing to recognise, every line must equal Poe's, and the flat line's twin is the purple one.

Option C · the pick

Cut-outs

Poe's sentences, cut at his own joints: the dash, the semicolon, the comma. Person or tense may change. Where he's silent, new lines are built from his words, under twelve of them.

The testCould it be a lost line of the 1845 text?

I'd pick Cbecause Poe speaks wherever he spoke, and there's a rule for where he didn't. The voice is cut like the look, from black paper, and its wordplay is made of cuts: the simple deceptions you asked for, leaning on the author.

Thread two

Depth in every element

The test. An element stays only if it does four things. It changes the player's next move. It returns three times: taught, turned on you in act one, handed to Madeline in act two. It gives the first-timer a decision and the Poe reader a recognition in that same decision. And it does something nothing else does.

A gotcha is a reveal with no next move. A chore is a move with no reveal.

The wordplay: the hard part

Of seven candidate mechanics, four died on the elements specialist's desk. Three reached the critique.

Option A · the pick

Echo

Madeline can only give back the end of what the guest said, and the end is where his denials come undone.

Only this taleRoderick's confession opens with an echo that strips a denial: "Not hear it? — yes, I hear it" [u48].

RiskSupply. It needs about a dozen great tails, one per turn and the ending; six are found.

Option B

Turn the word

Poe's double meanings as two-sided cut-outs: House, stones or twins; descent, lineage or going down; without the door, outside or lacking one; rest, repose or what's left.

VerdictThe richest vein with the weakest verb: find the lit word, turn it. Killed as a system; its finds feed the voice and the echoes.

Option C

The tarn reads backwards

In the water, words reverse: reined comes back denier, evil comes back live, pool comes back loop.

VerdictMerely cute as a system, a word search. Three are planted as the water's remarks instead.

the yet unscrewed lid.[u30] The Account's own line confesses. The screws line is struck, and the screw tips show through the wood.

On palindromes, honestly: the tale has two, eye and did. So the palindrome is one moment, not a system: did, which Poe italicised at her entrance [u49], is the one word the water can't change.

Thread three

The shadows, and new scenes

Option A

The Lamp

You hold the light, and what it throws on the wall is what the guest writes down. Held low, the physician's shadow climbs the wall, a villain.

RiskA dial, not a puzzle; it gives the physician's tell away; every horror game has a lamp.

Option B · the pick

A Mere Different Arrangement

You move cut-outs on rods before a lamp, and their shadows are what's there. Two gestures: drag a rod to place a thing; tap to bring it nearer the lamp, bigger and softer. Whatever moves without a rod is the house.

Only this taleIt's the narrator's own theory, that "a mere different arrangement of the particulars of the scene" might undo its sorrow [u02].

Option C

The Rods

Every figure is a puppet, and the water shows whose hand moves it.

RiskIt answers what should stay open (is the valet the house?) and makes every chapter puppetry.

I'd pick Bbecause a shadow becomes something you build with, so it makes puzzles rather than readings, and it crosses the waterline by a rule the look already keeps: below, light is floor. What you cast above, she stands on. What you hung to explain her away, she knocks at.

  • Where it recurs: taught in the first interlude (to hide her), then chapter II (to build her a way), the Haunted Palace (the lamp moves by itself), the burial (the bearers on the copper wall: three), the flight (the moon is the lamp), and every act-two interlude.
  • Act two's map, settled between all three: the shadow stage is the map and the Account is its key. Each front-page line stands there as the cut-out it named, a wall; each back-page item is a gap of light, a door. Her echo strikes a line, and its cut-out falls.

The Sleepwalker

chapter II · replaces the door's slide puzzle

Set the room. Her body walks above in a trance while she is below, so she passes "without having noticed my presence" [u14]. She walks on shadow, never on light; she turns at a wall; she climbs any slope a stair could be; at a drop she stops, arms out.

The first minute. The valet sets down the lamp: "The family never venture forth, sir." A gauze figure comes through the wall, arms out, walking the wainscot's shadow toward a gap of lamplight. Drag the chair, and its back's shadow bridges the gap.

Then, one idea per wall: lower the lamp and every shadow climbs; the house moves a piece without a rod; the floor shows her fall a moment early; and the choice, up to the window the long way, or down the bed's shadow to the vault immediately beneath.

Risk A borrowed genre (Lemmings, Sleepwalker). It lives or dies on walks under 40 seconds.

BelowA rod runs from her body down through the floorboards into the glass, where Madeline holds it. You walk the body while your recorded hand re-sets the room. Above, you set the stage for a walker you can't steer; below, you steer her on a stage you can't set.

The Haunted Palace

after III · replaces the one-minute collapse with about three

Roderick sings his ballad and you stage it in his toy theatre while the lamp creeps closer. You stage only the stanzas you earned: comfort keeps the palace fair, candour brings the lamp in a notch a stanza, and your spirits become "vast forms that move fantastically" [u25].

Then a shadow you aren't working crosses the screen and falls. It's her collapse, inside your show, and you took it for a puppet. Play on (Nevertheless.), or go round the screen and look.

BelowA minute behind the screen, mirrored: her hand on the rod of the body that falls.

Its Shadows Alone

chapter VII · replaces the flight, the weakest game

The setting moon shines through the fissure and lays a red road across the causeway. You run, keeping to the road. Whatever lies in the house's shadow is the house: the taps you hushed, your excuses, then her. Look back and they freeze, but the moon sinks and every shadow lengthens.

Today's flight walks for you: you hold one key while a counter ticks.

BelowThe house's shadow returns as light, so she runs on what chases you, and moves only while your recorded self looks back.
  • Folded or killed: the Pinhole (each screw she backs out opens a pinhole on the vault, inside the burial), and Held to the Lamp (a wall of text read backwards).
  • The games as played: the studio held and is the best thing built; the nights held; the tarn and burial are kept; the trist held but needs one fix, because nothing says the aim runs from the mace.
  • Timing: the Palace adds two minutes; trimming the gallery and library gives them back.

My read

Where I'd be careful

The shadow mechanism is now in six places

That's its strength and its danger. If dragging rods is fiddly on a phone, six scenes suffer at once. So milestone 2 proves it in one place, the first interlude, before it spreads.

Echo lives on supply

Six great tails are found; it needs about a dozen. If the next six aren't as good, she echoes only at the reveals and the knocks, never as filler. A weak echo is exactly the gotcha you warned against.

Red becoming hers alone is a real change

It rewrites the bible's red rule, and I think it's right. Three slanted reds on a phone screen collapse into "red is the answer", the oracle you rejected. As hers, it stays unreliable: below, she's the looker the water flatters.
