# The Arrangement

**Pitch:** Slide the stones of the House of Usher back into order; in the tarn below, the empty square you keep pushing around has a woman in it.

**Genre:** Haunted sliding puzzle.

**The screen:** The façade, ink on paper, cut into a 3×3 grid of stones with one gap; tap a stone to slide it. Below the waterline the black tarn holds the inverted twin, mirroring every move. One line of narrator text above.

**The first minute:** The façade is scrambled, "a wild inconsistency" [u06]. The narrator proposes the cure: "a mere different arrangement of the particulars" [u02]. Three moves in, the eye drops to the water: in the reflection the gap is not empty. A pale figure stands in it (Clarke's Madeline). Slide again; she moves with it. "The coincidence alone" [u40].

**The twist:** In a sliding puzzle only the gap truly moves. Here the gap is Madeline, and its solved position is the front door. Each stone carries a scrap of hairline that joins, when solved, into one zigzag from roof to water [u06]. The satisfying last move completes the fissure, opens the ebony jaws [u49] with her in them, and lets the blood-red moon through [u50]. The house sinks; the screen turns over; the house in the water stands upright, the real one all along, Madeline in its doorway. Stall or undo, and the tarn makes the next move for you.

**For everyone:** A puzzle anyone can play, a gasp when the hole turns out occupied, a solve that tastes of guilt.

**For connoisseurs:** The narrator's cure [u02] is Roderick's disease: sentience from the arrangement of stones and their reduplication in the tarn [u26].

**The 60-minute slice:** One SVG façade, eight stones plus gap, scrambled ~14 moves; reflection = same group flipped under a dark gradient, Clarke crop in its gap only; six explainer lines keyed to moves; the tarn moves after eight idle seconds; the ending (fissure, moon, halves sink, 180° turn).

**The risk:** Players who never look down miss her; the reflected gap must glow while the house's stays black.
