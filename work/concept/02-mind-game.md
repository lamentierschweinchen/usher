# Seven Differences

**Pitch:** A parlour spot-the-difference, the house above and its reflection in the tarn below, that cheats, won't count the woman in the water, and wakes when the pictures match.

**Genre:** Spot the difference.

**The screen:** One ink drawing on paper: the house, its inverted double in a black tarn, and a red-ink note: "Find the 7 differences." Click either picture. A wet red circle rings your find, and the reflection corrects itself. The narrator's marginalia sits below, one italic line.

**The first minute:** Three honest finds: a shutter, a dead tree, a chimney. Then it cheats. A circled find un-circles itself while your cursor is away. A pale figure appears in a reflected window. Click her and you get a grey cross: "Not a difference. A trick of the water." Each real find moves her one window up, toward the waterline.

**The twist:** Roderick believed the stones came alive through their arrangement "and its reduplication in the still waters of the tarn" [u26]. The puzzle is the spell. Stuck at six, you click the *empty* window above. The absence is the difference, and the rule reverses: the house copies the water. She steps up into the house. The pictures match; windows open like eyes, the door like a mouth [u22–u25]. The fissure runs from roof to drowned roof, the halves part, and the tarn closes [u50]. "Seven of seven. The house thanks you." Reload, and the page is upside down: "0 differences."

**For everyone:** A pretty, familiar puzzle that lies to you, then looks back.

**For connoisseurs:** It is the narrator's own experiment, "a mere different arrangement of the particulars of the scene" [u02], captioned with his explanations [u36, u40]. Twins made identical die of it [u30, u49]. Knowers click Madeline first and are told she isn't there.

**The 60-minute slice:** One SVG house drawn twice (upright and mirrored) with seven toggled details, plus clicks, ink circles, the counter, the rising figure, the reversal, and the split ending. Stretch goals: eye-tracking windows and reload memory.

**The risk:** If the drawing isn't handsome, it's a worse spot-the-difference app.
