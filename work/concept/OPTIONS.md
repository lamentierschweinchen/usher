# Concept: options for Lukas (2026-10-26, round two)

All four specialists independently landed on the same screen: the house above, its upside-down twin in the tarn below. The options differ in genre and in what the reflection does.

## 1. Seven Differences (02-mind-game.md)
**Pitch:** A parlour spot-the-difference between the house and its reflection, which cheats, won't count the woman in the water, and wakes when the pictures match.
**First minute:** Three honest finds (a shutter, a dead tree, a chimney), each ringed in wet red ink as the reflection corrects itself. Then a ring un-circles itself while you look away, and a pale figure appears in a reflected window. Click her: "Not a difference. A trick of the water."
**Only Usher:** Roderick says the house lives through its stones' "reduplication in the still waters of the tarn" [u26], so the puzzle is the spell. The seventh difference is an *absence*. Find it and the rule flips: the house copies the water, she steps up into it, and the house splits. Reload, and the page is upside down: "0 differences."
**Risk:** If the drawing isn't handsome, it's a worse spot-the-difference app.

## 2. The Arrangement (01-puzzle-box.md)
**Pitch:** Slide the stones of the House of Usher back into order. In the tarn below, the empty square you keep pushing around has a woman in it.
**First minute:** A scrambled 3×3 façade. Three slides in, you glance at the water: in the reflection, the gap isn't empty. Madeline (Harry Clarke's) stands in it, and she moves when you move it.
**Only Usher:** In a sliding puzzle only the gap truly moves. The gap's solved position is the front door, and each stone holds a piece of the fissure. Your satisfying last move completes the crack, opens the door with her in it, and turns the screen upside down: the house in the water was the real one.
**Risk:** Players who never look down miss her, and some people find sliding puzzles a chore.

## 3. Keeping House (04-toy.md)
**Pitch:** A cosy tidying game (A Little to the Left) in Usher: straighten portraits, shelve books, screw down the coffin lid, while the tarn below shows what you've really put away.
**First minute:** Tidy, click, tick. Then a tick unticks itself: the lid has slid ajar. You close it; it slides again. In the reflection, the lid is already off and the iron door stands open.
**Only Usher:** The narrator's cure for gloom is "a different arrangement of the particulars" [u02]; every tidy click inks the fissure, and the last chore is shutting the door on her. At 100%: "Everything in its place." The house sinks.
**Risk:** Without a really tactile snap it's mere drag-and-drop, and it's the most build of the three.

*Cut:* The Direct Line (03-deduction.md). Its ledger is the reading-and-choosing Lukas rejected, but its lore is worth stealing into any option: the Usher line stays branchless because one twin per generation goes into the vault.

**Coordinator's pick:** Seven Differences.
