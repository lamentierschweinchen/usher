# Keeping House

**Pitch:** Snap the House of Usher into perfect order, portraits, books, coffin lid, while the black water below shows what you have really put away.

**Genre:** Cosy tidying toy.

**The screen:** The house in cross-section, ink on paper: Roderick's studio, the guest bedroom, the copper vault directly beneath it [u29], standing on a black tarn that holds the house upside down [u02]. Drag crooked things until they click home; an IM Fell card ticks off the chores.

**The first minute:** Straighten a portrait: it wobbles, clicks, gets ticked. Shelve books, lift the guitar. In the vault, slide the coffin lid shut and tap four screws home [u30]. *Perfect.* Then a tick unticks itself: the lid has slid ajar. You close it. It slides again.

**The twist:** The tarn runs ahead of the house. In the reflection the lid is already off, the iron door hangs open, a white figure is on the stairs. Whenever something comes undone, a silent-film card gives the noise as a Mad Trist line [u39, u43, u46]; each time you put it back, the fissure gains a zig [u06] and the card praises you. The last chore: drag the studio door shut on her. At 100% the perfectly kept house splits into the tarn, card fully ticked: *Everything in its place.* (Stretch: tidy in the reflection, where moves reverse, and open the lid.)

**For everyone:** A Little to the Left with a ghost who keeps untidying, until you notice the tidying is the horror.

**For connoisseurs:** It's the narrator's experiment, a "different arrangement of the particulars" to cure the gloom [u02], and Roderick's theory that arrangement plus its "reduplication in the tarn" makes the house alive [u26]: your tidiness wakes it.

**The 60-minute slice:** One SVG; the reflection is the same group flipped, fed by a "truth" state one beat ahead. Eight snap objects, lid and screws, checklist, four-beat escalation (lid, iron door, stair, studio door), fissure, collapse.

**The risk:** Without a tactile snap it's mere drag-and-drop, and the water's first lie must be big enough to make players look down.
