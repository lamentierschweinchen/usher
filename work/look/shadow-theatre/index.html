Shadow Theatre

After Lotte Reiniger's Prince Achmed (1926): the whole House of Usher cut from black paper and held up against lamplight.

Palette · type · three lines

Three lines

  1. The ideaUsher as a Reiniger shadow play: house, trees and people cut from black paper with lace edges, held against graded lamplight from dusk amber to ink, and returned by the tarn's dark glass as light.
  2. Why it fits UsherPoe's narrator only ever sees the surface: walls, “vacant eye-like windows”, an inverted image in the tarn. A silhouette is a figure with no face, so the reflection, light where there was black, is the house's negative and its truth.
  3. The riskSilhouettes hide faces and furniture, so telling who is who, which room is which and what can be touched rests on profile and backlight alone; lace is slow to cut, gets lost on a phone, and turns to doilies or a cartoon if the edges are drawn instead of cut.

Rules: black is only ever paper; colour only ever light; nothing has an outline. Red is the one colour that sits on the glass rather than behind it.
