# Concept sprint: the game, the twist, the interface (Puzzle-box designer)

## The project
A small browser game from Poe's "The Fall of the House of Usher", made for a competition by Lukas, a writer and artist who makes the calls. He wants to blow the room away. The whole window is short: about 60–75 minutes of build remain after this sprint, for one capable builder working in HTML, CSS, SVG or canvas. Project home: `~/Documents/Usher`.

## What Lukas said about the first round (verbatim)
> "what do we want? a delightful game experience for anyone, but esp. literary connoisseurs who will be able to be delighted in ways the regular player won't. yet, of course the genre here must be somewhat recognizable and we need a visually pleasing experience. reading blocks of text and making decisions isn't that. we need to feel the spooky house of usher with its history, its secrets. so: what is the twist, the mirror, the upside down house — what is the simple-enough graphic interface that can carry the story? i don't think we're close yet although the ideas that came from the first runs can inform clever twists and turns of what we will end up building. back to the drawing board."

> "the book, unless there is a really clever literal interpretation that can be a delightful game, most likely will simply provide the frame for something fascinating, horrifying, entertaining, etc. we have and should take liberty and artistic license to bend the rules and not be beholden to faithful interpretations. in fact, the unfaithful can be the delight and the joke."

He also said: a game that "constantly tricks you and plays you as much as you play it" interests him; it must delight people who don't know the tale, and reward (or deceive) those who think they do; and sound can't carry the game unless it's music we can make in code.

## Raw material from round one (use, twist or ignore)
- **The tarn.** The house hangs upside down in black water; the narrator's first act is to look at the inverted house [u02, u05]. Roderick believes the house is alive because of the arrangement of its stones *and their reduplication in the tarn* [u26].
- **The fissure**, barely perceptible, from roof to water [u06]; the house splits along it at the end [u50].
- **Under the bed.** Madeline's vault lies directly beneath the narrator's bedroom [u29]. She is alive down there for days; Roderick hears her and "dared not speak" [u48].
- **The Mad Trist.** The narrator reads a cheap romance aloud, and each noise in it is answered by a real noise from below [u39–u47].
- **"The Haunted Palace"**, a poem in which a palace is a head: windows are eyes, the door is a mouth, the throng rushes out laughing [u20–u25].
- **The explainer.** The narrator explains everything away ("merely electrical phenomena", "the coincidence alone", "the rushing gust") and is right about every detail and wrong about the woman.
- **One name, one line.** "House of Usher" means family and mansion [u04]; a line with no branches, father to son. Twins [u30].
- Things Lukas found weak: blocks of text plus choices; mechanics that need good audio; anything whose hook only works if you know the tale.

## Your lens
You design tactile visual puzzle objects: The Room, Monument Valley, Gorogoa, Windosill. Your instinct is one beautiful object that opens in surprising ways, with mirrors, inversions and impossible geometry. The house and its upside-down twin in the tarn are your natural material.

Three other specialists are working other lenses in parallel. Commit to the best concept from yours; don't hedge across genres.
