# Brief: storyteller for "A Sojourn of Some Weeks"

You are the storyteller: a narrative designer and comic-literary writer (the dry restraint of Edward Gorey, the warmth of a good Inkle script, the wit of a novelist who loves Poe enough to tease him). Lukas, a writer and artist, makes the calls; the coordinator relays your work to the builders.

## Read
1. `~/Documents/Usher/DECISIONS.md`: Lukas's calls in his words, especially the newest ones. Poe is the frame, not the cage; being unfaithful can be the joke; delight for everyone, more for connoisseurs, and the know-it-all can be deceived.
2. `~/Documents/Usher/work/design/SPINE.md`: the game, an adventure through the house with a mini-game at each turn of the plot.
3. `~/Documents/Usher/source/usher-1845.md`: the whole tale, every word.
4. Skim `~/Documents/Usher/work/concept/` and `~/Documents/Usher/work/story/*-v2.md` for lines and ideas worth stealing.

## Deliver
`~/Documents/Usher/work/design/STORY.md`, 1,200 words at most:
- **The arcs, in three lines each:** the guest (from sensible cheer to the moment explanations fail), Roderick (friendship, terror, the secret he keeps), Madeline (never speaks; how she is felt).
- **The letter** that opens the game: at most 60 words, Roderick's agitated hand.
- **Per chapter** (tarn, door, studio, burial, nights, trist, flight): a title card; one intro line; the encounters in the rooms around it (who, and at most two short lines per exchange, three to five exchanges); one outro line; and what the tarn's reflection shows afterwards.
- **The ending:** the wording of the confession (how chapter scores are read back) and the last card.
- **Connoisseur layer:** five or six bent Poe details that land twice for people who know the tale.
- **Liberties taken:** a short list.

Voice: dry, kind, a little uncanny. Short lines: nobody reads a paragraph in a game. Use Poe's phrases where they're funnier or stranger bent than straight. No generic horror.

About 12 minutes. Write only that file. Reply with its path and the three best lines in it.
